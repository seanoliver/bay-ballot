RECOMMENDED CANDIDATES
Prior to the June primary, Indivisible Sonoma County members supported the following endorsements:
Mike McGUIRE AND Mike THOMPSON ENDORSED
Indivisible Sonoma County has endorsed State Senator Mike McGuire in his race in California’s first congressional district.
✓ YES McGuire, a Sonoma County native, is well-known here, although the new CA-01 district will extend far beyond the county, both north and east. He has extensive experience in the state legislature, where he has termed out, and presents himself as a progressive fighter. We believe he will be an energetic opponent to the fascist caucus across the aisle and reform-minded on many of the key changes we need to see to our political system, such as expanding the Supreme Court.
Indivisible Sonoma County has endorsed Congressman Mike Thompson in his race in California’s fourth congressional district.
✓ YES Thompson is well-known in the county and Indivisible Sonoma County has met with him and his staff since our inception. He is an able legislator and a senior member of the critical Committee on Ways and Means. We’re pleased to see him promising to pass single payer healthcare, if given the majority.
In these challenging times, we’re also pleased to see him with a sharpened fighting spirit and more willing than he has been in the past to press the attack on Republicans. We hope that he will bring that same energy to pushing the House leadership to do better.
Thompson’s challenger didn’t impress us as being ready for the job.
We’re hoping to help give Thompson a majority and expect him to bring the same vigor that he has shown in this campaign to this upcoming term in Congress.
Additionally, although the membership did not consider an endorsement, Indivisible Sonoma County encourages a vote for Jared Huffman in the California 2nd Congressional District.
RECOMMENDED MEASURES
✓ YES
Sonoma County Measure H: Sonoma County Parks Sales Tax
Measure H extends Measure M, approved by Sonoma County voters in 2018, a ⅛ cent local sales tax, which funds our regional and city parks.
✓ YES
Local Measure D: Coast Life Support District Parcel Tax
Funding for Measure D will help ensure the continued availability of emergency ambulance and life support services in a large, geographically-challenging rural service area where rapid access to emergency medical care is essential.
✓ YES
Sonoma County Measure AB: Sonoma County Junior College District Improvement bond
The quality of educational facilities and equipment is a big determiner of the quality of education. This is a measure to issue $830,000,000 in bonds to improve facilities that will provide training to students for careers in practical job-related fields. Measure AB will cover all five SRJC campuses; it will be on the Sonoma, Marin, and Mendocino County ballots. Passage requires 55% voter approval.

RECOMMENDED PROPOSITIONS
✓ YES California Proposition 1:
Affordable and Veteran’s Housing Bond
$11.25 billion bond measure to provide funding for affordable housing.
✓ YES California Proposition 2:
Budget Stabilization Account Cap Increase and Gann Limit Changes Amendment
Allows state policymakers to save more in strong revenue years to better protect vital public services during
economic downturns, since deposits in the key reserves would no longer count as spending for the Gann
Limit calculations.
✓ YES California Proposition 3:
Makes permanent existing taxes on high earners for education and healthcare.
Makes permanent two previously approved income tax measures on the highest income earners in the state.
✓ YES California Proposition 4:
The California Fair Elections Act
This measure, if passed, would repeal the current ban on public campaign financing – allowing any city or county, or the state, to implement one of a list of public financing systems for candidates who opt in to not take large contributions from anyone.
✓ YES California Proposition 5:
Changes Recall Election Process for 5 Statewide Officers.
Makes one basic change to the recall process: voters would decide whether to remove an elected official
separately from deciding who replaces them.
✓ YES California Proposition 37:
Creates Loan Program for Middle-Income Buyers of Qualified New Homes.
Creates affordable homeownership opportunities for working and middle-class Californians and encourages
new home construction—with ZERO COST TO TAXPAYERS.
NO California Proposition 38:
Authorizes Bonds for Immunology Medical Research.
Authorizes $8.4 billion in general obligation bonds for immunology and immunotherapy research, half to a
single University of California-affiliated nonprofit medical research institute, half to research grants. The
billionaire founders of that non-profit are co-sponsors of Prop 38 and have written the proposition to so
narrowly define the criteria for the $4B that only their non-profit is eligible.
NO Proposition 39:
Prohibits Citizens From Voting Unless They Present Government-Issued Identification.
This initiative, if passed, would intentionally create hurdles for all voters, increase confusion, and depress the
vote. Prop 39 is a proposed Constitutional Amendment, placed on the California ballot by
MAGA Republicans and billionaires. Make no mistake: Prop 39 will make voting harder, riskier, and more
expensive for Californians because that’s what it was designed to do.
✓ YES California Proposition 40:
One-Time Wealth Tax for State-Funded Health Care Programs Initiative.
Right now, families across California are one hospital closure away from losing the care they depend on.
Federal healthcare cuts have blown a $30 billion hole in Medi-Cal funding, and Prop 40 is our chance to fix it
directly: a one-time 5% tax on the roughly 200 Californians worth over $1 billion. It’s projected to raise nearly
$100 billion over five years, 90% for healthcare, the rest for food assistance and education.
NO California Proposition 41:
Prohibit Excluding New State Taxes from Spending Limit and Require Special Tax Audits Initiative (2026)
NO California Proposition 42:
Prohibit New Taxes on Retirement Holdings, Personal Assets, and Savings and Limit Retroactive Taxes Initiative
Propositions 41 and 42 aren’t independent policy proposals; they exist to cancel out Proposition 40. Under California’s rules, if either measure gets more “yes” votes than Prop 40, the billionaire tax could be nullified even if it also passes. That’s the entire point: they were placed on the ballot by Building a Better California an organization co-founded by Sergey Brin and Eric Schmidt, which has raised over $118 million specifically to defeat the wealth tax.
NO California Proposition 43:
Limits Voters Ability to Raise Revenues for Local Government Services.
Starting , the California Constitution would be amended to require a 2⁄3 vote to pass citizen-initiated local special taxes. This would replace the current system that allows citizen-initiated local special taxes, such as for schools, roads, public safety healthcare, and community services, to pass with a simple majority vote.
NO California Proposition 44:
90% Healthcare Spending for Non-Profit Safety-Net Clinics
Prop 44 could worsen health disparities rather than reduce them, and would potentially put safety-net clinics, which serve low-income and uninsured patients, out of business, because it requires affected clinics to pay a penalty to the state if they fall short of the new health care spending minimum.
NO California Proposition 45:
Changes to Environmental Review Process for Certain Projects Prop 45 shifts power from public agencies to developers and nearly eliminates the courts’ ability to prevent toxic contamination of air and water.
