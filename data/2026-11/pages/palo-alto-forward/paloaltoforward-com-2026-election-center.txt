Judging a Pro-Housing, Pro-Transit Candidate
Candidates often say things that make judging who really supports pro-housing, pro-transit policies difficult. PAF cannot endorse candidates, but we can provide pointers to help you identify candidates aligning with PAF’s values. Pro-housing candidates will support:
All housing, not just “affordable housing”: Elected officials sometimes use affordable housing as a pretense to block housing. Creating a Palo Alto accessible to everyone means supporting housing of all kinds—affordable, market-rate, and everything in between.
State housing laws encouraging more housing: Pro-housing elected officials understand that state laws supporting more homes compliment local efforts. “Local control” is not inherently good or bad; the state and our city can work together to create a community with homes for everyone.
Expanding renters’ rights, not just sticking to the status quo: Palo Alto has strengthened its tenant protections in recent years, but it still has weaker policies than nearby communities like Mountain View and Redwood City. Pro-tenant candidates support new policies.
Bike lanes, wider sidewalks, and slow streets: Creating a pro-mobility city means supporting safe options for people to move around by every kind of transportation. That includes cars, but isn’t limited to them.
Candidates’ voting records also indicate where they stand. AI tools can help identify and compare the candidates’ records with the following prompt: “Compare and contrast the housing records of candidates running for Palo Alto's city council. Cite specific quotes from websites or forums as well as their concrete voting records if they have them”—though you have to check their sources!
