Housing on the Ballot this Election
Your vote matters! Palo Alto’s last City Council election was decided by just 10 votes.
Learn about pro-housing and pro-transit ballot measure endorsements, candidate forums, and voter registration details.
Vote NO on Prop 43: Howard Jarvis Taxpayer Deception Act
Kneecap local governments’ ability to raise revenue for essential services and housing.
Endorse NO on Prop 43
Vote YES on RTM : Regional Transit Measure to Connect Bay Area
Protect Caltrain, BART, VTA, and other transit agencies from severe service cuts.
Endorse YES on RTM
Vote YES on Prop 1: Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond Act
$11.25 Billion for affordable housing production and preservation, building up to 40,000 new homes.
Endorse YES on Prop 1
Vote YES on Palo Alto Measure J: Fund Cubberley Community Center
1/2-cent sales tax to fund community services and safety, including upgrades to Cubberley Community Center!
Vote YES on Measure J
Our endorsements reflect state, local, and regional housing priorities. We have not taken a position on any ballot measure not listed here.
PAF does not usually take positions on state ballot measures. However, Palo Alto also has plans to build homes on city-owned parking lots downtown. The Measure P campaign in Menlo Park represents an anti-housing playbook that will be used to block affordable homes in our own community if not soundly defeated in November.
City Council Candidate Forum on HOUSING
Learn where the eight Palo Alto city council candidates stand on housing near transit, opportunity in exclusive single-family neighborhoods, protections for renters, and more. Co-hosted by Palo Alto Forward and the SV@Home Action Fund.
