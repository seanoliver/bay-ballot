OUSD School Board
Oakland’s school district has a leadership crisis
A city’s public schools impact every resident, family, and neighborhood. When school board leaders fail to govern, our entire community pays the price.
Oakland Unified School District (OUSD) spends more per pupil than almost every large district in California. Yet, only about 1 in 3 met state reading standards, and just 1 in 4 met math standards. OUSD’s problem is not a lack of money or dedicated educators on the ground – it’s the lack of a vision and plan for fiscal solvency, which is squarely the responsibility of the school board and the superintendent it hires.
Here’s what the current school board’s majority has done in just the past two years, led by President Jennifer Brouhard (D2) and VP Valarie Bachelor (D6), both of whom are up for re-election:
Pushed out the experts: Fired a well-respected superintendent without explanation, drove out the Chief Business Officer, and spent nearly $900K on outside consultants to cover the gap.
Budgeted blind: Approved layoffs, labor agreements, and major financial decisions without calculating what they would cost or save. Critical budget items were often buried in no-discussion “consent” votes, which nearly eliminated after-school care programs despite warnings from the district’s own experts.
Backroom deals: Quietly made the interim superintendent permanent without conducting a promised public search, then handed out executive pay raises.
Disrupted citizen oversight: Attempted to divert voter-approved career readiness tax dollars to pay for basic operating expenses, and left district committee seats vacant while qualified candidates went unanswered.
Ignored school leaders: More than 60 principals and administrators publicly called out the board’s “fundamental misunderstanding” of what schools need, after haphazard cuts left staff covering basic operational gaps across a sprawling, underutilized footprint of 78 district-run schools.
Oakland’s kids deserve better. Our teachers and principals who are constantly being asked to do more with less deserve better. We must elect leaders capable of building a real plan and executing it.

We recommend ranking Arielle Fleisher #1, and leaving the #2 spot blank.
Arielle Fleisher is an OUSD parent, a PTA member, co-leader of her neighborhood council, and founder of a 300+ member moms group. Before becoming a candidate, she helped build an independent parent-led guide to the OUSD budget, so families could actually engage instead of tuning out. In many ways, she started doing the work of a school board member long before she ran to be one.
She’s spent her career working on public systems that people depend on, from managing vaccine clinics to advancing affordable transit and street safety. An avid biker and a product of public schools, she is an active leader in both local and state politics, currently serving as an elected delegate for the California Democratic Party. We have also seen Arielle’s dedication firsthand: she has volunteered with Empower Oakland since its earliest days, most recently as a core team member and policy lead focused on the problems OUSD faces before stepping back to run, and she consistently goes the extra mile to deeply understand a situation before recommending a solution.
As an OUSD board member, she wants an open and transparent search for a permanent superintendent, budgets based on fiscal reality, and experienced teachers who can actually afford to live in Oakland.
Notably, Fleisher is running against current board president Jennifer Brouhard, who has presided over OUSD’s tumultuous decline since she was elected in 2022. Fleisher would be a welcome addition, and much needed breath of fresh air, to a board in desperate need of overhaul.

[Barbara Lee]
Barbara Lee
Support Barbara(opens in a new tab)
It’s easy to forget what Oakland looked like in 2024. A city council that was at war with each other, a mayor that was indicted by the FBI, and a crime epidemic spiraling out of control.
Mayor Lee has brought steady leadership and righted the ship, laying the foundation for Oakland’s comeback. She has effectively brought together many constituencies to the table and taken the temperature down in local politics. Early signs show that she has successfully “done no harm” across public safety, homelessness, economic development, and housing. When contrasting this with Oakland under our prior mayor, there is no question that Barbara Lee deserves appreciation and support.
Many may remember that we did not support Barbara Lee in the 2025 special election. Some of our concerns remain. At times, we wish the mayor moved more quickly and decisively. Her desire to build a big tent also meant that she often has too many cooks in the kitchen on major decisions. If the Strong Mayor charter reform (Measure EE) passes, we are hopeful she will move more aggressively on Oakland’s key issues. No matter what, we support her administration as a step in the right direction for Oakland and are optimistic about the city’s trajectory.

BART Board, District 4
[Robert Raburn]
Robert Raburn
Support Robert(opens in a new tab)
Before Robert Raburn ever held office, he spent a decade chairing Alameda County’s Measure B Citizens Watchdog Committee (the body that makes sure voter-approved transportation dollars actually go where voters were told they’d go). He came to this work as a watchdog first, and he has governed like one ever since.
As a professional transportation planner, with training and experiences that directly relate to being a successful board member, he championed the “Fleet of the Future” order that finally retired the last legacy BART cars in 2023, insisting the new trains include dedicated space for wheelchairs and bicycles. He helped drive BART’s transit-oriented development policy, which has already delivered 275 units at Fruitvale (including BART’s first supportive housing for veterans), 110 at Coliseum, and more than 250 affordable units in San Leandro.
Robert also consistently shows up with his community. He is a fixture at neighborhood meetings, station walk-throughs, and community events across his district. His connection to his constituents is something to be recognized and it shows in how he can speak about specific conditions at individual stations in this district.
You might hear:
“He’s been on the BART board for 16 years. Isn’t it time for new blood?”
Usually we’d agree that long tenure without a serious challenge is how boards get complacent, but BART is weeks away from one of the most consequential election votes in its history, followed by either a major rebuild or deeper service cuts than the system has ever made. Now is not the moment to hand District 4’s seat (with some of the most at-risk stations in the system) to someone learning the budget from scratch. Raburn’s institutional knowledge, fiscal discipline, and real relationships in these neighborhoods are assets we cannot afford to throw away this cycle.

Measures
City of Oakland
Yes on Measure EE
Charter Reform: Strong Mayor
Empower Oakland endorses YES on Measure EE because Oakland needs clear accountability at City Hall.
For too long, responsibility for the performance of city government has been divided in ways that make it difficult for residents to know who is actually accountable when basic services fail. Measure EE will make the position of mayor a true chief executive role, with clear responsibility for managing city affairs and delivering results. If the streets aren’t clean, public safety isn’t improving, or departments aren’t performing, Oaklanders should know exactly who is responsible.
Just as importantly, oversight stays strong. The city council would retain its legislative and budget authority, gain confirmation power over key department leaders, have the ability to override mayoral vetoes, issue subpoenas, and establish an independent Budget and Legislative Analyst's Office.
To ensure that accountability holds, we strongly urge the mayor and city council to restore full funding to the Public Ethics Commission, the independent body responsible for investigating elected officials. With a stronger mayor’s office, the more important those checks and balances.
You might hear:
“This puts too much power in one office.”
It puts responsibility in one office. Oakland currently spreads responsibility too thinly across too many bodies so that nobody is truly accountable. We believe effective government starts with clear authority, clear responsibility, and no more excuses about who is in charge.
City of Oakland
Yes on Measure FF
Real Property Transfer Tax
Empower Oakland endorses YES on Measure FF.
Currently, when a property in Oakland changes hands, the buyer pays a one-time transfer tax to the city. But there’s an exception: when a bank or investor takes ownership of a property after the owner defaults, no tax is owed.
Measure FF ends that exemption for banks and big investors. It is not a new tax, and it does not raise anyone’s property tax rate. The exemption stays in place for properties converted to transitional and interim housing, for transfers within a family, and for community banks under $10B in assets.
The city expects this to bring in $4 million to $13 million a year.
Regional
Yes on Measure RTM
Regional Transit Measure
Empower Oakland endorses YES on Regional Measure RTM.
Oakland’s economy cannot recover if transit collapses. AC Transit expects to generate roughly $50M a year from this measure. If the measure doesn’t pass, the agency’s own contingency plan cuts service by at least 16%. Moreover for BART, the contingency plan would be to close 15 stations and cut services by as much as 70%.
We do not endorse new taxes lightly. But this one carries real accountability:
Independent efficiency review of the four largest operators
Continued funding conditioned on the cost-savings plans submitted
Tax that sunsets in 2041
With vehicle ownership costs pushing more people onto public transit, the region needs this safety net. It buys stability, but it doesn’t substitute for governance. Vote yes, then hold these boards towards more accountability.

How We Make Endorsements
Our endorsement process is thorough and deliberative. A volunteer committee of everyday Oaklanders creates detailed questionnaires for each race, invites all ballot-qualified candidates to respond, and conducts in-person interviews for key races. For ballot measures, we review the full text, independent analyses, and arguments on both sides. All endorsements require a 60% committee vote, and members recuse themselves from races where they have personal conflicts.
