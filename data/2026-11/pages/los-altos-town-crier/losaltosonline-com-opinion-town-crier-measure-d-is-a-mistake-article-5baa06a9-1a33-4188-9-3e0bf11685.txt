Town Crier: Measure D is a mistake
Town Crier Editorial Board

After countless conversations, letters to the editor and weekly reporting, the Town Crier editorial board has found three main problems with Measure D.
The first is that Measure D is unnecessary. Citizen initiatives are fundamentally a last resort when government has been unresponsive to the needs of the citizens it represents. It’s hard to argue this was a necessary last resort while elections go unopposed, and board and commission seats go empty. We have a system that works pretty well if you get involved. You just have to raise your hand and participate.
The second is that Measure D was poorly drafted. Clearly, the idea behind Measure D was to put to a vote any theater, park or parking garage project on the parking plazas in downtown Los Altos. While the merits of that are debatable, that is not how the measure was written. Instead, the text of Measure D was written in such a way that it impacts our Housing Element and therefore the mighty State of California. If the measure had included the phrase “except housing,” we would be discussing what to do with the parking plazas instead of how to avoid lawsuits, Builder’s Remedy and rezoning.
Finally, passing Measure D is the exact opposite of getting a more responsive government and locally controlled planning. Some might believe that Measure D gives the people a voice; that once the people have spoken, elected officials and city staff will have to listen to them. It doesn’t work that way. Instead, if Measure D passes, staff and elected officials will have less time to spend listening to what Los Altos residents want because they will have to spend more time listening to what lawyers, real estate developers and bureaucrats in state government want.
While the specific impacts of Measure D remain cloudy – lawsuits, Builder’s Remedy, Housing Element recertifications, zoning changes – one thing is certain: Mitigating those impacts will be done by elected officials and city staff at the expense of doing something else for the city. There is only so much time in the day. Do we really want city staff spending their days doing damage control?
The point of Measure D is well taken. Our community is changing and the residents want a say in how that change happens. Fair enough. While we agree with that premise, Measure D doesn’t actually do that. Instead, it just makes things harder for Los Altos.
Let’s defeat Measure D and move on to the thoughtful, honest conversations about the direction of our community we all deserve as neighbors.

CathyLu
The editorial argues that Measure D is unnecessary because elections go unopposed and commission seats go empty. Based on what I have seen in this Measure D fight, I would say it takes extraordinary courage for someone who is not a member of Los Altos’ “ruling elite” to run for council. The “No on D” campaign has been incredibly well organized. Their responses to posts online and in print have been quick and often unkind. Take for example the deplorable treatment of the Draeger family who have served Los Altos for some 40 years with their market downtown. The opponents assume that anyone who defends the downtown parking plazas must have an ulterior financial motive. This behavior is more telling of the accusers, and the public should be wary of that.
Is Measure D poorly drafted? It is true that Measure D was motivated by the desire to save our parking plazas and have the council pause their plan to build a parking garage, theater, and park and reconsider. Throughout this campaign, the council has shown no interest in reconsidering their plans or to acknowledge in any way that they have been arrogant in their approach to citizen input. What Measure D will do if passed is force the city to sell its ideas to the community since a vote is required. This means making a strong case for proposals, explaining the pros and cons both in terms of intangible benefits and budgets. That is not happening today. This is why it was possible to get some 3000 voters to sign a petition in a few weeks this past April.
The editorial’s suggestion that the measure should have included the phrase “except housing” would have accomplished nothing. We will still be watching our special downtown being slowly destroyed.
If Measure D passes, would elected officials have less time listening to residents? I submit that our city council and staff’s time are and will continue to be consumed by dealing with our convoluted and oppressive state housing law, prospect of YIMBY lawsuits, and opportunistic developers in this hyper pressured development environment. Will it be easy to fight the state housing laws? Of course not. Should we just roll over and let the state dictate its vision of California? I don’t think so. The Menlo Park Council just voted unanimously to reject the 80 Willow Road skyscraper project, even after receiving a threat from Attorney General Bob Bonta’s office. This is a problem cities and towns up and down California are dealing with. Instead of working with their peer cities, Los Altos appears to be a lone vote against the League of California Cities’ Murrieta Resolution which is to set up a taskforce to study how to gain back more local control in housing matters. Why? It seems like our present council members are housing advocates. Advocacy for a good cause is admirable, but it is not the appropriate role for the stewards of our city.

billshepp
The "deplorable treatment of the Draeger family" is classic "follow the money". He has closed all of his stores except for Menlo Park and Los Altos. He has backed both measures. He closed and sold his San Mateo grocery, which is now under construction as a large development. The value of his 1.4 acres on First St will rise significantly if Measure D passes, because the City will be forced to rezone for higher density and taller buildings. And if Builder's Remedy is imposed (a real possibility) he'll profit to an even greater degree. But Los Altos residents will lose when an over-size project gets built on First St. All of this also applies to Ron Packard, another out-of-town property owner backing Measure D (whose actions in large part led to the five-story project currently under construction at 40 Main).
Is Measure D poorly drafted? Absolutely, without question. It doesn't define "affordable housing", which completely hamstrings the City's efforts to build it on Plazas 7 & 8. It doesn't exempt ADA or EV upgrades or short-term leases of parking spaces to PG&E, CalWater, or other utiltiies who frequently need a staging area for maintenance projects. And, inexplicably, it hands full legal control of Los Altos' response to the inevitable lawsuits to Measure D's authors, providing a green light for them to appeal losses, no incentive for them to settle, and no accountability to Los Altos taxpayers, who will pay for every lawsuit (both sides if we lose).
You state "the council has shown no interest in reconsidering their plans". They are currently in a thoughtful, deliberate planning process for a possible park. Why should they cut short this process well in advance of a final design, which would still require City Council approval before any changes are made? Just to appease a strident minority who want to maintain acres of asphalt in perpetuity?!? That's not democracy, that's naked populism. And it's not a good look.
You state "Measure D will ... force the city to sell its ideas to the community since a vote is required." No, Measure D will end any development on the parking plazas. A public vote is simply incompatible with sound city planning processes. Planning non-trivial projects requires lining up partners and financiers. No partner will devote significant time and effort to a project which after a year or more of design will still be subject to the vagaries, cost, and delays of a public election. Measure D protects acres of asphalt in perpetuity, regardless of the likely future need to make adaptations.
You wrote "it was possible to get some 3000 voters to sign a petition in a few weeks this past April.". The opponents of Measure D have spoken to many of these voters who now regret their decision. They feel misled over the true intent and the ugly ramifications if it passes.
You wrote "The editorial’s suggestion that the measure should have included the phrase “except housing” would have accomplished nothing." If Measure D had included "except housing" it would have prevented the park proposal. The proponents have made many claims that Measure D is about parks, not housing. Your statement shows the true intention of Measure D - to prevent housing as well. Measure D is Deceptive.
Menlo Park's rejection of the Willow Rd project will likely result in millions being spent by MP taxpayers. We've seen this story in Los Altos (40 Main and 999 Fremont) and are nearly $6M poorer as a result. And Los Altos was far from the only city to oppose the Murrieta Resolution. It was a voice vote, so it gets recorded as a single "nay" vote since the actual vote count isn't known, but recordings of the meeting verify that there were many who were opposed. And with good reason, according to the discussion of this vote at our City Council meeting.
