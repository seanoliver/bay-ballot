Endorsement: Pass Measure CC hospital tax; elect Su, Hess to healthcare board

With the goal of providing quality healthcare from an independent hospital focused on Marin residents, leaders of MarinHealth Medical Center and the elected Marin Healthcare District board members are trying to forge a path to financial stability amid skyrocketing costs.
The November ballot includes choices that could shape the hospital’s future. Marin voters, excluding Novato, face Measure CC, a 14-cent-per-square-foot parcel tax on improved building area annually for the next 30 years. It is estimated the tax would raise about $12.4 million annually, or $372 million over 30 years. It needs a simple majority to pass.
Also, candidates are running for seats on the district board in Division 1 (eastern San Rafael) and Division 3 (Kentfield, Ross, Corte Madera, Larkspur, Mill Valley and Greenbrae).
The money from Measure CC is designated for the expansion and modernization of intensive care, trauma, triage and hospital facilities, as well as additional capacity for the emergency room and patient rooms. The funds may also be spent to hire and retain doctors and nurses, with no funds for administrator salaries.
Dr. Brian Su, who seeks reelection in District 3, is running against Dr. Jonathan Goff. Su, the co-medical director of spine surgery, has been an integral part of hospital decision-making for nearly 20 years. He says residents asserted the desire for a quality independent hospital and MarinHealth cannot get there without money from the tax.
“There is no other way to pay for the improvements and care that residents of the community said they wanted when it became independent after Sutter,” said Su, referring to Marin Healthcare District taking the lease of the hospital from Sutter Health in 2010.
Dr. Clayton Hess is running for the open Division 1 seat against nurse Nancy Hoepper, who leads the program at Dominican University. Hess is the official opposition to Measure CC in the voter guide. The San Rafael resident runs a practice in Nevada County. He says the proposed tax asks for too much and that the fiscal oversight system needs an overhaul.
“It is a conflict of interest to have the same person as the head of the board and the nonprofit running the hospital,” Hess said. “Without changing governance, we can expect more of the same. Taxpayers will be called up to rescue the hospital again and again.”
Leadership says issues in the emergency department and with an upcoming deadline to retrofit the remainder of its Greenbrae campus for earthquake safety make the tax crucial.
It is true that the state has mandated that Redwood Pavilion must meet safety standards soon. Supporters say a plan exists to begin work quickly if Measure CC passes.
If work is not done in time and Redwood closes, the hospital would lose 63 patient beds. That is especially problematic considering the increased demand for emergency care. Supporters say emergency care has increased by 22% since 2022. Leaders say the tax would be used to expand the ability to care for increased load.
Hess says the tax is about wants, not needs.
“There has to be another way,” Hess said. “This 30-year ‘mortgage’ will be a burden for taxpayers and our children.”
Like Su, Hoepper and Goff support Measure CC. Hoepper says she elected to buy a home in San Rafael’s Canal neighborhood in order to stay in the socioeconomically challenged community she is committed to helping.

“We need a collaborative patient-centered effort on the board,” she said of her candidacy. “In my time with Dominican, I have developed community initiatives to establish pipelines to Marin Community Clinics. … I have developed partnerships and pipelines that get our students to MarinHealth to help fill vacancies.”
Goff said he would bring an independent voice to the board with the goal of evolving it into a group operating with fiscal discipline, accountability and transparency.
Supporters of Measure CC are right, MarinHealth needs another push as it tries to gain steady footing in providing independent quality care. The IJ editorial board recommends voting yes.
But more aggressive oversight is needed. Dr. Brian Su has the institutional knowledge and the details down. He knows how much it will cost to get the hospital to where we want it to go. Su and other board members are clearly being pushed by Dr. Clayton Hess, who has done his homework and brings fresh ideas.
Along with recommending Measure CC, the editorial board supports reelecting Su, while also endorsing Hess so his taxpayer-centric views can be heard.
