Endorsement: IJ recommends Beckman, Ravasio, Fruin for Corte Madera council

Until recently, the Corte Madera Town Council appeared to be conducting the public’s business without much turmoil.
That was until Councilmember Pat Ravasio wound up in the crosshairs of criticism from some of her colleagues; complaints that she had divulged confidential information about a legal issue from the council’s closed-door meetings.
Participants in those meetings are legally required to keep details of its “executive session” deliberations confidential, although the council is also required to report publicly that it is meeting, the reason for the closed-door session and any votes taken.
While that dustup was building steam, the council canned long-term town manager Adam Wolff, drawing complaints from residents and little explanation from council members.
In the wake of that tumult, the council has hired an attorney to look into Ravasio’s misstep, which she calls “an inadvertent mistake.”
Her “mistake” and Wolff’s ouster have put a spotlight on the council and made public transparency a prominent issue.
Ravasio has welcomed the investigation. The controversy, she says, has been a lesson learned for the entire council.
That’s the lead-up to town voters’ decisions to choose three council members from a field of five good candidates.
Ravasio is seeking a second term. Councilmember Eli Beckman, who made an unsuccessful run for state Assembly, is now running for a third term on the council.
And Rosa Thomas is stepping down after serving one term, leaving an open seat on the ballot, which includes three first-time candidates – local schools trustee Ava Fruin, deputy state controller Deborah Gallegos and retired civil engineer David Bell.
The town is fortunate to have five candidates seeking office.
Our recommendation is retaining Beckman and Ravasio and electing Fruin.
Beckman has been a strong member of the council since he was first elected in 2018. Beckman, who grew up in Corte Madera, first ran for council as a voice for its younger residents and families. He also said the town needed to be better prepared for a shift in the retail industry, from the brick-and-mortar stores on which the town gets much of its tax revenue, to online business.
He was right.
In his run for Assembly, he was ready for larger challenges, but since that race, he’s decided he has more to accomplish in town.
Beckman, an architect, has also become a leading voice in guiding town’s efforts to meet the state mandate that it pave the way for building more than 700 new homes by 2031.
Ravasio, a real estate agent, was a longtime civic volunteer before her election to the council. On the council, Ravasio is a community cheerleader who champions civic improvements and programs such as upgrades to its parks, the town’s creation of a multi-generational center and opening the Redwood High School pool to the larger community. They may be seen as lighter-weight issues on council agendas, but often they are important to many constituents and without a champion they can fall off the town’s list of priorities.
The best councils have a balance of perspectives and experience.
Fruin, among the first-time council candidates, brings her experience in having served on the Larkspur-Corte Madera School District and being a parent raising her three children in town.
The council’s job is much broader than the school board. Its decisions directly affect a lot more people.
But Fruin has experience serving as an elected representative and public decision-maker.
At this junction, the council needs that experience and her connection with the town’s younger households.
David Bell is a longtime resident and community volunteer. He has served on the town’s flood control board for 10 years.
On the pressing challenge of meeting housing quotas, he admits he still has homework on the issue.
His call for greater community communication should be heeded by whomever is elected.
Gallegos, deputy in the state controller’s office, has experience in public decision-making. She knows the rules and responsibilities and says change is needed on the council.
Her experience is in a different level of government; not neighborhood issues that mostly fill council agendas.
She could use more experience in having served in local or town offices.
Still, her candidacy has been endorsed by North Bay Labor Council.
Voters in Corte Madera have a broad range of candidates from which to choose three. Each could do the job.
The IJ editorial board’s recommendation is to retain Beckman and Ravasio and elect Fruin to the town council.
