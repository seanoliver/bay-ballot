The future of Marinship is usually a front-and-center issue when it comes to candidates for Sausalito City Council.
With Measure X, the initiative rezoning and establishing land-use standards for the 200-plus acres of what is mostly a former World War II shipyard sharing the ballot, the race for two seats is no different.
Two veteran council members – Joan Cox and Jill Hoffman – face two challengers – Nastassya Saad and Adrian Brinton.
We recommend voters retain Cox and Hoffman, but both Brinton, an author of the Measure X initiative, or Saad, a city planning commissioner, are candidates who have done their homework and would be knowledgeable council members.
Cox is a municipal lawyer and is serving her second term on the council. Her resume also includes having served eight years on the city Planning Commission.
“I know how the city works and I know how to get things done,” she told the IJ editorial board.
Hoffman is seeking her fourth term. She is an attorney and served in the U.S. Navy Judge Advocate General Corps. She is a captain in the Navy Reserve.
Both are strong members of a politically balanced council.
Both are opposed to Measure X. The two lawyers are stressing that the devil is in the details – the initiative’s wording – and that the council is already working on a plan to address many of the goals and concerns raised in Measure X.
In fact, of the four candidates, only one supports Measure X – Brinton, one of its authors.
Saad, who grew up in Sausalito, says she’s worried that Measure X opens the door to a “pro-development intent” that’s not in scale with residents’ vision.
Brinton says Measure X lays the foundation for turning the waterfront into a vibrant hub for local businesses, commerce and the community. It also ends a 40-year stalemate over its future, he says. The revenue it generates can be used to make needed drainage and street repairs, as well as address the city’s other budget needs.
Cox was a strong voice in support of the 2025 city-sponsored measure that re-zoned part of the city-owned Martin Luther King Jr. school property for affordable housing. Her knowledge of municipal law and the experience she draws upon from her years of service in Sausalito and her work with other municipalities makes her a valuable voice on the council.
She says she supports the construction of new housing “that fits the town,” especially second units.
Cox adds that she’s interested in creating a business improvement district for the Caledonia Street area.
Hoffman is outspoken and, at times, contrarian to her council colleagues. She can be the sand in the sunscreen.
That’s what makes democracy work. That’s its inherent strength. It may be harder to reach a consensus when council members differ, but democratic decision-making is supposed to represent a broad range of perspectives and concerns.
That may not make life simpler for city staff or for other council members.
Hoffman is not shy about speaking her mind. She also proved that she’s not timid when it comes to rocking the boat.
Sausalito now has a council that reflects a broad range of perspectives. That strength should be valuable as the council faces significant issues such as sea-level rise and the state’s ambitious housing quotas.

They do not all agree on how best to chart the future of Marinship, but regardless of whether Measure X passes, this council will be leading the decision-making. Measure X will return control over land-use decisions to the council, eliminating a 1985 voter-imposed requirement that zoning changes need voter approval. If Measure X fails, the council is already working on updating the city’s vision for this important commercial and community site.
Saad and Brinton are both well-spoken and well-informed candidates. They care about the city and its future. Saad’s experience as a planning commissioner is a plus.
They don’t have the years of experience and knowledge that Cox and Hoffman bring to the council and its decisions.
Faced with the important task of recruiting and hiring a new city manager, their experience is even more important.
The IJ editorial board recommends the reelection of Joan Cox and Jill Hoffman to the Sausalito City Council.
