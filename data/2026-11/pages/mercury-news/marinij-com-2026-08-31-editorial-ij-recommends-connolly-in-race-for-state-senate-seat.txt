Endorsement: IJ recommends Connolly in race for state Senate seat

Veteran Marin politician Damon Connolly enters the race for ’s election of the North Coast’s new state senator with an impressive advantage.
The San Rafael Democrat was the top vote-getter in the June primary. Connolly, who has served two terms representing Marin and Sonoma counties in the state Assembly, received 73.4% of the vote in the three-candidate race to succeed Mike McGuire, who is serving his third and final term.
Connolly, the only Democrat in the race, was the top vote-getter in every one of District 2’s seven counties.
His Republican challenger on the general election ballot is Tief Gibbs, a Novato businesswoman with a conservative agenda that leans far right from the majority of the district’s voters.
She says she’s running to provide “an alternative” to the state’s current Democratic administration.
She’s running to promote that agenda, which calls for getting a lot tougher on crime, establishing camps in eastern counties for homeless people to voluntarily go to get help they need for their problems they have with drug abuse and mental health issues, and banning transexuals from girls and women’s high school and college sports teams.
Her governmental experience is limited. She serves on Novato’s Downtown Business Association.
On the other hand, Connolly’s agenda includes a stint as a state prosecutor and serving in elective office on what is now the Miller Creek School District board, the San Rafael City Council and the Marin Board of Supervisors.
He was elected to the state Assembly in 2022.
That’s an impressive resume of public service and civic representation.
The experience and knowledge gained from having served as a decision-maker in those positions clearly makes him the best-qualified candidate. He’s faced the challenges of reducing homelessness, of dealing with illegal immigration, the funding of public schools, land-use planning and fighting crime.
He’s a trusted leader and representative. In June, voters in the two counties he represented in the Assembly gave him better than 70% of the vote.
In Marin, he got an 82.1% majority while Gibbs received 13%.
Connolly may not be the kind of politician who is quick to leap onto a stump and champion an issue.
He has, however, made legislative headway on some important issues.
In Sacramento, he worked to end the misguided and costly bike lane trial on the Richmond-San Rafael Bridge and advanced tougher safety rules for e-bikes and youths who ride them. He also passed legislation increasing safety requirements for fencing surrounding school campuses. He says he also wants to continue to work on wildland fire protection and insurance reform.
While Gibbs says Connolly is “soft on crime,” the lawmaker correctly points to the endorsements he’s received from law enforcement unions in Marin and Sonoma counties.
Gibbs is also an outspoken critic of the state’s housing push that has eroded local land-use standards. The state has turned over too much power to developers, she says, pointing to three tall apartment complexes on the drawing boards for downtown Novato. They are “completely out of character and out of step with Novato,” she says.
Many Marin voters would agree.
Gibbs says that if elected she would push to repeal those pro-developer laws.
Connolly agrees that the state laws have paved the way for development that exceeds long-established building height and reasonable density limits, but he notes that strategy has strong support in Sacramento.
While he says he has spoken out about state housing laws allowing out-of-scale building and unrealistic state housing quotas, we would like to see him speak out a lot louder.

Rather than accepting party leaders’ warning that proposed reforms have little luck of getting a fair hearing, we want to hear Connolly speak out, advancing the need to rebalance the process and reform unrealistic top-down housing mandates.
Connolly says he will push for those reforms in the Senate.
“People know I’m willing to stand up and deliver on the biggest issues,” Connolly said
Clearly, voters in June said that they “know” Connolly and they prefer him to represent them in the Senate.
In this race, voters are getting to know Gibbs beyond Novato. Besides a lack of political experience, her conservative tilt on immigration, gun rights and abortion show that she’s marching to a different drummer than most of the district’s voters.
The IJ editorial board strongly recommends Damon Connolly to represent and serve the Second District in the state Senate.
