It has been awhile since there has been a competitive race for a seat on the San Rafael City Council.
The race for the council’s District 3 seat is a very close call with Daryoush Davidi, a local businessman, and Robert Sandoval, an attorney and county planning commissioner, vying to succeed Councilmember Maribeth Bushey, who has served since 2013.
Sandoval and Davidi would both bring impressive strengths and experience to the job. Either one could do the job.
Davidi’s stronger connection with San Rafael’s business community would fill a lacking need on the current council.
District 3 is a geographical stretch including most of the San Pedro Peninsula, part of downtown, the Dominican neighborhood and stretching over Puerto Suello Hill to include a southern slice of the North San Rafael area.
The conversion to district elections was supposed to encourage more people to run for office and stir more competition. So far, it hasn’t.
Some voters may be looking for a candidate who would stridently oppose the high-rise out-of-scale housing complexes that seem to be flooding the city’s planning agenda. Recent changes in state land-use laws and state-awarded density bonuses that bypass locally set limits and requirements have undermined local control.
Neither voiced any criticism of the city’s recent approvals. Both stressed that San Rafael needs the housing, although Sandoval says some of the buildings “are too tall” and he would press for the city to adopt planning standards that he says could give it more say.
Sandoval, also a leader of the Point San Pedro Road Coalition and member of the city’s Parks and Recreation Commission, says he wants to bring “a new generation of leadership” to the council.
Many San Rafael voters may remember him from his unsuccessful 2024 bid for the local seat on the Marin Municipal Water District board.
This is Davidi’s first bid for elective office.
Davidi, whose produce-import and packaging company is headquartered on Kerner Boulevard, has been active in the San Rafael Chamber of Commerce and is a member of the city’s newly formed San Rafael Police Advisory Committee.
Both candidates are critical of the city’s behind-the-scenes approach in its plans to turn an old school campus on Merrydale Road into a city-run homeless camp. The plan drew an outcry from neighbors that they were “blindsided” by the city’s plans.
The campus is in the Third District.
Both candidates agree the city should have done much more to inform and involve neighbors in its plans, but they are supportive of the project.
In the wake of the Merrydale controversy, Davidi says he supports City Hall making “greater outreach to neighborhoods” a priority.
Sandoval also says the city needed to bring neighbors into the planning process much earlier.
Effective measures for keeping residents and businesses informed about city initiatives and decisions needs to be a higher priority than it has been.
Both stress the need for more housing and its importance in supporting and growing San Rafael’s economy.
Davidi says the city needs to take advantage of the growth of the SMART train as potential for improving downtown’s vibrancy.

Sandoval says the city should try to bring in an “anchor business” to downtown, stressing the city needs to become “investible” again to attract new businesses.
Both say they don’t blame people for being fed up with homeless encampments, but the Merrydale project offers an alternative and an environment where people can get the help and safer housing they need.
Sandoval says the city needs to take a tougher stand regarding those who don’t take up offers for alternative housing or services.
On Measure W, the city’s ballot proposal to raise its tax on local real estate sales, Davidi said he’s still undecided.
Sandoval, at our editorial board meeting, said he opposed it, calling it “a huge tax” and that the city should instead be encouraging the turnover of homes. The next day, he reversed his stand and said he’s supportive of Measure W, saying it would provide needed funding for city fire protection services.
San Rafael is going through a lot of change, high-rise housing complexes and the multi-phased conversion of the Northgate mall into a larger housing and retail complex.
Daryoush Davidi’s business perspective and the need for City Hall to reflect local business’ importance to San Rafael’s economy, provides him with a very slight edge in getting the IJ’s recommendation on the ballot.
