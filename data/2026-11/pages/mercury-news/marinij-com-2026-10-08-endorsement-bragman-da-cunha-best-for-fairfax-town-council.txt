Over the past two years, hometown politics in Fairfax has been contentious. It has been marked with a battle over rent control, the ouster of two council members in 2024, a third narrowly missing being unseated in that contest and a recall election that the targeted council members survived — but a challenge that deepened the split in town.
The town’s handling of a proposed six-story apartment complex proposed for School Street – a project taking advantage of new state housing mandates that eroded local planning standards – hasn’t bolstered citizens’ trust in Town Hall.
On the ballot, Fairfax voters are being asked to elect two new council members.
Incumbents Stephanie Hellman and Lisel Blash, the targets of last year’s recall, are stepping down.
Six candidates stepped forward for those seats. Two – Aubrey Harmon and Cheryl Scorza – have dropped out of the race, although their names still appear on the ballot.
The four active candidates are attorney Susan Adams, former San Anselmo Councilmember Doug Kelly, retired firefighter Matt Da Cunha and former Mayor Larry Bragman.

Bragman’s candidacy follows those of longtime council members Frank Egger and Mike Ghiringhelli, who came out of retirement to run in 2024 and won.
Bragman, who was on the council from 2003 to 2014 and served two terms on the Marin Municipal Water District board, says he’s ready to return to “help bring the town together.”
His election – with his experience and knowledge of the town and the community – could help accomplish that need.
Toward that same end, Da Cunha is well-suited for the job, having grown up in Fairfax, reared his family here, being retired from a career as a firefighter and having coached Little League and youth soccer teams. That’s a breadth of experience and community connections that should prove valuable to the council, especially at a time when it has to hire a new town manager.
As a veteran firefighter he has fought wildland fires that have destroyed towns and neighborhoods. His fire-safety perspective would be additionally important.
“I will work to bring people together, bridge divisions, and lead with empathy and common sense,” he says in his ballot statement.
If there ever was a time for civic bridge builders in Fairfax, it’s now.
All four candidates preached greater civility, including among council members. Some say the lack of civility among the town citizenry is because many people are frustrated and that they don’t feel town staff or council members listen to them. There is a big difference between being listened to and having prevailing opinions.
But failing to recognize and respect others’ opinions – both among the citizenry and on the council – is where civility frays. It fuels calls for change, reforms and recalls.
Adams and Kelly are also both good candidates. A former state attorney, Adams’ legal and bureaucratic expertise could be helpful. Her resume of local experience, however, is not as deep as other candidates.
Kelly is stepping down after having served three terms on the Ross Valley Sanitary District board. He was unsuccessful in his bid for a Fairfax council seat in 2024. He also has been an active member of the Marin Coalition of Sensible Taxpayers, a watchdog group that often locks horns with local governments.
His interest in continuing his public service is admirable, but he may not be the right fit at the right time for Fairfax.
Kelly says the Town Council should organize with other cities and towns to fight Sacramento’s housing mandates and their undermining of local control. The School Street project is “not right for Fairfax,” he says.
When it comes to meeting the state quota of building 490 housing units by 2031, Adams is the most pro-housing of the group. While others question whether there’s available space and building opportunities to meet that quota, Adams says the town should promote adding accessory dwelling units and building smaller homes.
Bragman says he’s not sure the town can build its way out of its “affordability crisis,” but he says progress can be made by taking advantage of building opportunities when they arise – such as the construction of the Victory Village affordable housing for seniors – and homeowners adding ADUs.
On the ballot, Fairfax voters have four competent candidates from which they will elect two. Larry Bragman and Matt Da Cunha stand out as the two candidates the town needs at this time.
