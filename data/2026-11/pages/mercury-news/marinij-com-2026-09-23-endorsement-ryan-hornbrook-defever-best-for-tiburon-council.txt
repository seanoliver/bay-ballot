With issues of traffic, infrastructure, sea-level rise and housing in full focus for an engaged populace, three newcomers join two incumbents running for three seats on the Tiburon Town Council in the November election.
Incumbents Alice Fredericks and Jack Ryan are joined by Kathleen Defever, Faris Jafar and Chuck Hornbrook in the race. Mayor Jon Welner’s seat on the council is expiring and he is not running for reelection.
Ryan, a financial risk expert who retired from Wells Fargo, joined the council in 2020. Defever, a member of the Tiburon Planning Commission, is a consumer insurance attorney involved in helping wildfire victims. Hornbrook is a business adviser and member of the Tiburon Parks and Open Space Commission.
The two newcomers and Ryan bring a complementary set of expertise that appears most likely to help the town tackle its biggest challenges.
Ryan says he is committed to transparent governance while prioritizing communication with all town residents. On top of that, his professional financial expertise has clearly been valuable to the town.
“I want to continue to make the town government and its services more available to the public,” said Ryan, who calls himself a consistent supporter of park improvements for residents.
Before joining the council, Ryan ran the Yellow School Bus county program in the Reed Union School District, which continues to be promoted as a strong tool in fighting traffic congestion on Tiburon Boulevard. Ryan called the bus program a good tool in the toolset for addressing the issue, which continues to light a fire under town residents.
All candidates called for continued positive communication with Caltrans and the Transportation Authority of Marin as solutions for congestion on the town’s only true evacuation route emerge.
Defever’s time on the Planning Commission has been steeped in housing mandates from the state. She has worked through approvals for potential sites, weighing development plans with impacts to emergency services and evacuation routes. As president of the Tiburon-Belvedere Rotary Club, she has used her platform to organize informational events for homeowners faced with uncertainty as catastrophic wildfires continue to impact the insurance industry.
“We need better planning and we need to make an effort for more cooperation with our fire departments,” Defever said. “We are not ready at all for an emergency on the Tiburon Peninsula.”
Like the other candidates, Defever is in favor of coordinating countywide plans to address sea-level rise. She was disappointed by improvement plans put in motion by the neighboring community of Belvedere without Tiburon’s input. Considering the shared shoreline between the two, she called the decision “ludacris.”
Calls for improvements to parks and open space have been a priority for many residents in recent years and Hornbrook’s position on the parks commission put him in a position to help keep the ball rolling. The Tiburon Parks Plan is lauded as a promising guideline for management and upgrades.

“I helped turn the Tiburon Parks Plan into timely, actionable results,” Hornbrook said.
As the only candidate with young children attending Reed schools, Jafar served on the district revenue advisory committee and helped develop self-funding mechanisms to help increase school resources. He advocates for increased “housing stock” with the belief that more residents will help downtown businesses thrive.
Fredericks has served since 2001, representing Tiburon capably on regional boards. She has relationships with elected officials in Sacramento and Washington. Fredericks is a generational leader and is endorsed by several elected officials, including Rep. Jared Huffman and Marin County Supervisor Stephanie Moulton-Peters.
However, the IJ editorial board is generally in support of term limits and it is time for leaders with fresh ideas on the council. Regardless of what happens in November, Fredericks should be celebrated.
In this election, the IJ recommends Jack Ryan, Kathleen Defever and Chuck Hornbrook in the race for seats on the Tiburon Town Council.
