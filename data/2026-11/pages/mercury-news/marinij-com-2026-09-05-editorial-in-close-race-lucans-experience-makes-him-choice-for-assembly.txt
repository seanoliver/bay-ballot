Endorsement: In close race, Lucan’s experience makes him choice for Assembly

The race for the open seat as Marin’s representative in the state Assembly is a close call.
June’s hard-fought six-candidate scrap for the seat left vacant by Assemblymember Damon Connolly’s decision to run for state Senate left two Democrats to face off on the November ballot – Marin Supervisor Eric Lucan and Rohnert Park City Councilmember Jackie Elward.
Both enter the race having drawn significant support from voters in their home counties.
The 12th Assembly District, represented by Connolly since 2022, includes all of Marin and a large slice of Sonoma County – from Petaluma north to the south end of Santa Rosa.
In June’s contest, Lucan received 28.3% of the vote and Elward 22.6% to reach November’s general election.
Where the votes that went to the other three Democrats and the lone Republican, who finished third with 19.4%, go will determine this election.
Given his deep experience in leadership positions in Marin, including the bi-county Sonoma-Marin Area Rail Transit board, Lucan brings the most political leadership to the race. Elward offers her experience in Sonoma County and her inspirational and impressive story as an immigrant from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the important perspective as a minority.
She aligns as the more “progressive” candidate and her campaign has the strong backing of labor unions and 12 members of the Assembly, Connolly among them.
Lucan also has union support, but he also has the backing of local business groups. Significantly, he has endorsements from a large majority of Marin officeholders, among them, Rep. Jared Huffman, three fellow Marin County supervisors – Stephanie Moulton-Peters, Brian Colbert and Mary Sackett – and by far most of the local city and town council members – 45 of the 55. Impressively, he also has the endorsement of two county supervisors from Sonoma County.
The depth of Lucan’s support reflects his local experience and local leaders’ respect for him as a thoughtful and hardworking leader and representative.
What divides the two candidates is experience.
Lucan grew up in Marin and has been involved in local politics for more than two decades. He has served as a council member and mayor of Novato. In 2022, he was elected to the county Board of Supervisors. The breadth of endorsements from Marin leaders is a reflection of his knowledge of Marin and his experience in addressing local issues.
He is present and he shows up.
His business experience as chief marketing executive for Mike’s Bikes, a large chain of bike stores, is also important.
As a county supervisor, he’s dealt with the broad range of issues that will be on the legislature’s agendas in Sacramento. The challenges and need for action when it comes to affordable housing, homelessness, climate resiliency, public health and safety-net support are not new to Lucan.
Combine that with his local knowledge and experience, Lucan has our recommendation.
Lucan is a strong supporter of building housing, especially the affordable kind. But he’s seen the effects of the state Legislature’s erosion of local control over land use and the ramifications of density bonuses that are leading to developers’ plans for taller buildings that are out of character with reasonable community standards.
We expect that Lucan, who says the Legislature’s strategies have given too much power to developers, will become a staunch and outspoken advocate for revamping Sacramento’s initiative and restore greater local control over the size and design of housing developments.
As a council member and a supervisor, Lucan has a reputation for being careful and considering possible short- and long-term unintended consequences.

Both candidates are critical of the last-minute political attacks on Elward during the June campaign. They were financed by an out-of-district political action committee launched by crypto billionaires who are taking aim at union-backed candidates. Elward says she’s expecting more of the same during this campaign and says district voters should not let “outside money” influence their decision.
Lucan says the advertising blitz was “unjust” and “a disservice to our community.”
Elward has proven herself to be a strong member of the Rohnert Park council. The city’s work in police reform and building housing to help reduce its local homeless population is admirable.
But the breadth of Elward’s governmental experience and knowledge of Marin, more than half the district’s population, and its local issues is limited compared to Lucan.
Lucan has proven himself to be a careful, caring, responsive and responsible local political leader. The IJ editorial board endorsed him in June and we stand by the endorsement as we approach the election.
