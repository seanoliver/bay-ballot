Endorsement: Novato should reelect Picus to school board

Her experience as an educator, having three children in Novato public schools and having served four years as a trustee make Abbey Picus the clear choice for the Novato Union School District board.
It’s not an easy job and some tough decisions face the school board.
Picus’ experience and knowledge are going to be valuable to the board as it navigates difficult financial shoals confronting Novato’s public schools.
Her challenger is Francis Droulliard, a longtime resident, retired engineer, a member of the city’s police advisory board and a Marin GOP stalwart.
He says he decided to run after meeting young people while working at the Republican Party booth at the Marin County Fair. He said he found them aware of social issues, but lacking knowledge. He says schools need to restore their focus on “core educational values.”
It’s a familiar “back to basics” mantra that is often sounded by critics of our public schools. Today’s educators are also faced with the challenge of both providing students with an educational foundation while also providing them with lessons that are relevant and will serve them today and in the future.
Drouillard’s answer is that the district should encourage retirees to return to classrooms as volunteer instructors and mentors.
District leaders need to think “outside the bubble,” he says.
The two are running for the board’s Area 4 seat, which includes the northwest part of the district.
Picus knows the job is more complex, dealing with issues as varied as public health; mental health and safety concerns; recruiting and retaining top-quality teachers; repairing and maintaining district campuses; and, of course, providing the community’s youth with a solid education and academic opportunities.
A large issue facing the school board is renewing and increasing its annual parcel tax after a June ballot measure failed, falling short of the two-thirds majority needed for passage by a fraction of a percentage. The fact that well over a majority of Novato voters supported the measure – one of the lowest asks in the county – should be a strong argument for the district bringing back to voters.
Unfortunately, until that happens, the district has to close a $6.5 million budget shortfall.
Droulliard’s candidacy is helping keep a public focus on this challenge.
Droulliard says he opposed the June measure, but says he’s convinced there is a need for the local revenue the tax generates. He says he would like to see the tax revamped so that instead of being a flat parcel tax, it is based on the square footage of properties. Property owners with larger homes would pay more.
He says he would also support a 2% annual increase, tying the escalator to the limit allowed under Proposition 13.
Unfortunately, that shift has been hard to sell to voters. The Ross Valley School District tried and failed, only to return with a traditional parcel tax which won voters’ endorsement.

Novato needs to return to voters with a sure thing – a measure that already has won a large majority – not a risky revamp.
Also, focusing on the nexus of the tax, do the owners of larger homes benefit more than those with small homes when it comes to public schools?
Droulliard also says the school board should consider closing an elementary and middle school campus, while Picus says closing one or more of the district’s campuses won’t close that budget gap.
Picus says the district needs to do a better job of informing voters. “We just need to simplify the information and make sure we’re getting it out there,” she said.
In Picus, the school board has an educator who knows the local public schools from the perspective of being a parent and from being a classroom teacher who’s working with students every day.
There’s a breadth of experience and community perspective on Novato’s seven-member school board and Picus should be reelected to help maintain that strength.
