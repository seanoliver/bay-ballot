Ross is a small town with a big political split.
Bob Herbst and Paul Roesler are on the ballot to reverse the town’s decision to close its firehouse, and swing decision-making their way.
While they contend having firefighters stationed in town is critical, the council has relied on fire officials’ advice that townspeople will also get prompt emergency response from stations in San Anselmo and Kentfield. Also, a Ross Valley Paramedic Authority team is stationed in Ross.
The majority of emergency calls for fire departments these days are for medical attention.
Herbst and Roesler challenge the argument and that the safety of town residents deserves the fastest possible response by remanning the firehouse.
Herbst was a leader of the group that led a petition drive to put the issue on the ballot. The group dropped its initiative after the Town Council agreed that rebuilding the firehouse at the corner of Sir Francis Drake Boulevard and Lagunitas Road would be part of its plans to renovate the municipal center there. But the council has held up any commitment to return a team of firefighters to the station and that decision has reignited critics.
It is a costly proposition and important decision.
Veteran Councilmember Julie McMillan, who is seeking reelection, says the annual tax could be as much as $4,000. She says that is “a heavy, heavy burden to bear.”
In addition, the tax would need voter approval.
Last week, Town Treasurer Jeffrey Kuhn was appointed to fill the seat on the council that became vacant after the departure of Bill Kircher. Kuhn’s appointment lasts through 2028. He is still listed as a candidate on the ballot for one of the other two seats, but he is no longer eligible to win.
Town voters have choices to make. There are two council seats on the ballot and three good eligible candidates. Longtime Councilmember Elizabeth Robbins is retiring after serving three terms.
Reelecting McMillan and electing Herbst is a balanced approach. Having Herbst on the council will provide a voice and vote for his side of the debate.
It may not make for smooth civic sailing, but it does strike a balance, providing a large constituency with a vote in town decision-making.
McMillan is currently mayor and, with Robbins’ retirement, the other council members bring only two years of experience to the post.
McMillan is a lawyer and a longtime civic leader, including having served a term on the Ross School Board. Her knowledge of how town government works and her community experience are a value to the council.
She is also currently the chair of the Ross Valley Fire Department board.

Herbst has lived in Ross for 14 years and works in construction management and development. He is involved in civic efforts in Ross and in San Rafael.
He and Roseler – a top executive in a large investment firm and a leader of the board of the Ross School Foundation – don’t hold back when criticizing McMillan’s leadership.
They argue that Ross is bearing more than its fair share of the fire district’s budget. Roesler says the town is paying 20% of the fire district’s budget, but its share of the district’s population is only 10%. He says the town might get better protection and a better price by forming an agreement with Kentfield or Central Marin fire departments.
Herbst says the town needs to renegotiate its RVFD contract. That could significantly bring down the town’s cost and reduce the estimated cost of a town tax to reopen the firehouse, he says. Herbst says the town should bring back its firetruck (now used by Ross Valley) to roll to local emergencies from a renovated and upgraded Ross firehouse.
A hurdle in that strategy is that the fire department’s board members from San Anselmo, Fairfax and Sleepy Hollow have to be open to renegotiation.
Meeting the state-mandated housing quotas, undergrounding of utility lines and, of course, flood protection are also priorities raised by the candidates.
As McMillan said, Ross is not a one-issue town.
In this race, voters have to choose from a field of three strong civic-spirited candidates. McMillan and Herbst offer a balance and breadth of civic experience and perspectives.
