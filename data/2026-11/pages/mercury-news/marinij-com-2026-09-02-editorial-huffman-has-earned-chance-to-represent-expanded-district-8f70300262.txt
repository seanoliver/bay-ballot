Jared Huffman has represented Marin and the North Coast in Congress since 2013.
He’s been an advocate for the environment, commonsense gun control, wildfire protection and the communities that he represents. The San Rafael Democrat has also been a sharp and staunch critic of President Donald Trump, reflecting the politics and significant concerns for a large majority of his constituents.
In the November general election, he’s seeking reelection to an eighth term. But due to last year’s voter passage of Proposition 50, his district was redrawn and now also includes several counties – Shasta, Modoc and Siskiyou – along California’s eastern boundary.
Huffman is up for the challenge, including the addition of those Republican-leaning counties.
Their addition was evident in the results of the June primary, where Huffman, who in recent elections has received more than 70% of the vote, drew seven challengers. He still finished first, but with 56.6%.
His Republican challenger, June’s second place finisher on the November ballot, is Shasta County school trustee and business owner Robin Littau. Her finish was boosted by being the top vote-getter, by far, in her home county.
But she’s got a steep uphill fight to unseat Huffman, who won overwhelming victories in his district’s three most-populated counties – Marin, Sonoma and Humboldt. In Marin, for instance, he got 75.8% of the vote.
His leadership and record of representation are well-known.
As he says, he works hard and “shows up.”
He’s also an outspoken critic of the Republican-controlled Congress capitulating to Trump while sidestepping its constitutional role of political checks and balances. He says many families in his district are facing “an affordability crisis,” fueled by an “illegal war” in Iran and Trump-led trade and tariff wars that are driving up costs for businesses and consumers.
Littau, in her meeting with the IJ editorial board, was not a fire-breathing Trump sycophant. Rather, she said she supports immigration reform instead of mass deportation and is not a full-throated backer of the president’s war in Iran. She says “a lot of people are more centric and don’t want to see chaos.”
Littau says the mass-deportation strategy lacks compassion, but she supports “swift deportation” of illegal immigrants who commit crimes.
She says she opposes “extremist” politics and socialism and is running to bring “accurate representation” to the Second District.
How Littau’s moderate politics would function in Congress, where partisan lines have rarely been deeper, is a huge unknown. At the very least, she would face a sharp learning curve, including getting to know Marin and other parts of her district.
Huffman is a well-known quantity, especially in Marin and Sonoma where he served on the Marin Municipal Water District board and represented the two counties while serving three terms in the state Assembly and seven terms in Congress. He’s proven that he can represent the varied and sometimes conflicting interests in his district.

He also has a sharp knowledge of the politics and legalities affecting tribal lands and rights, including those concerning gambling and housing. When called up to get involved in a federal issue involving his district, he plays an active role.
Huffman’s a sharp critic of the White House having turned the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement into a cruel and costly “paramilitary force” to carry out its agenda to deport millions of illegal immigrants.
Huffman is outspoken. At the same time, he’s been criticized by the political “far left” for not championing their views. His role in the closure of 12 historic dairy and cattle ranches in Point Reyes National Seashore has drawn criticism from some in Marin’s agricultural industry.
There is, however, agreement among a large majority of his constituency that they want someone representing them in Congress who will challenge the White House’s penchant – as Littau politely put it – for “chaos.”
For Huffman, he says, it’s a matter of upholding the Constitution and our nation’s rule of law.
He was there in Congress during the violent attack on our government and governance. He knows and understands what’s at stake.
The IJ recommends the reelection of Jared Huffman to represent the Second Congressional District.
