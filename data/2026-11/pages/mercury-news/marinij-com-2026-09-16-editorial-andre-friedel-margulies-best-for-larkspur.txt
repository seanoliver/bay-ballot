Endorsement: Andre, Friedel, Margulies best for Larkspur

On the ballot, Larkspur voters have to choose from a field of six strong candidates.
Councilmember Catherine Way, who has been on the council since 2013, is stepping down, leaving one open seat. The other two council seats on the ballot are held by incumbents Stephanie Andre and Sarah Margulies.
Both have only been on the council for two years, having won election to fill mid-term vacancies.
There are four challengers, Jeanne Friedel, Brian Jaffe, Aaron Burnett and Gareth Walter.
Jaffe and Walter are building their campaigns on Larkspur residents’ frustration over the long-vacant Lucky’s store in downtown.
Jaffe, an independent home builder, says the council needs “a culture change” and cites the vacant Lucky’s as well as complaints about the city’s building permit process as examples.
Walter, a former Salesforce executive who now serves as president of a Marin music education nonprofit, touts himself as “new energy.” He has pushed for the city to adopt a vacancy tax levied on corporate property owners who leave their buildings vacant, such as Lucky’s former downtown store.
Among the challengers, Friedel offers the most City Hall experience, as an 18-year member of the city parks and recreation commission and a volunteer in the successful effort to raise more than $5 million in donations that helped make the city’s new library a reality. Among the challengers, her learning curve would be the shortest.
Burnett grew up in Marin and works as an immigration attorney for San Rafael’s Canal Alliance. He says he would bring the perspective and concerns of younger voters to the council.
He, Jaffe and Walter would be stronger candidates if they had experience serving on other city panels, such as the Planning Commission.
Andre, Margulies and Friedel are solid experienced choices for the council.
Andre, a former investment banker, has brought her business acumen to the council. She hit the job running. She focuses on short- and long-term costs when it comes to city spending and investments.
As the city’s representative on the MCE (formerly Marin Clean Energy) board, she has rightly questioned the public power agency’s investments and procedures. She is cautious, she does her homework and she’s not afraid to question established policies and practices.
Margulies, the lone Greenbrae resident on the council, is a former vice president of a tech firm that specializes in digital engagement platforms. She didn’t have a ton of local civic experience when she ran for council in 2024 and she has had to learn a lot about the city, City Hall and local government’s varied responsibilities and challenges.
She’s a stronger candidate today. Most important, her perspective as a longtime Greenbrae resident is needed on the council.
The longstanding vacant Lucky’s site and housing are two top issues facing the city.
The vacant market is an understandable source of complaints from residents. It is a large gap in downtown commerce.
Both Andre and Margulies say it is a top priority for the council.

Among the hurdles facing the city is that a special tax on vacant commercial buildings would require voter approval and the cost of holding that election.
The city faces a state-mandated quota of approving construction of 979 new housing units by 2031.
Andre says the city needs to build “a coalition for growth” to find ways to meet that mandate.
Jaffe is critical that the city has not kept up with its need for housing and says it should promote construction of accessory dwelling units citywide.
Margulies says the city’s approval of housing on the long-vacant Ross Valley Sanitary District property near Larkspur Landing is a big step toward meeting the city’s quota.
Larkspur voters are fortunate to have six solid candidates willing to step up and serve the community.
Each could do the job.
On the ballot, the IJ editorial board recommends retaining incumbents Stephanie Andre and Sarah Marguiles and electing longtime civic volunteer Jeanne Friedel for Larkspur City Council.
