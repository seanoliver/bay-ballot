Editorial: Bishop-Mello and Zickuhr Give Brentwood the Reset It Needs
By Mike Burkholder
by CC News
written by CC News
[Brentwood]
L to R: Tammy Zickhuhr & Holley Bishop-Mello
87
Brentwood used to be one of the gems of Contra Costa County.
“Better in Brentwood” actually meant something.
Now? Some days it feels more like “Everywhere Else Is Better Than Brentwood.”
That may sound harsh, but look around.
Residents are frustrated. Businesses get inconsistent treatment. The Planning Commission has gone rogue at times. City Hall has lost experienced staff at an alarming rate. The council has made questionable decisions, followed by more meetings to figure out the unintended consequences of those questionable decisions.
This isn’t progress. It’s government whack-a-mole.
Then there is the political culture surrounding Brentwood.
Speak out against council behavior or challenge the direction of the city and suddenly the conversation isn’t about policy anymore. More than two dozen people can tell stories about online attacks, personal vendettas and even deepfake videos.
Funny how those deepfakes never seem to target Mayor Susannah Meyer or Councilmember Jovita Mendoza — Hmmm.
Meanwhile, Brentwood has lost too many employees over the past two years to list here. Instead of stabilizing City Hall, the council has made hiring decisions that could take years to unwind. The recent city attorney hiring was the cherry on top, and we’re already seeing reasons to question whether that was the right move.
Which brings us to this election.
Residents need to think long and hard about what they want Brentwood to look like for the next few years.
Because with Meyer and Mendoza remaining on the council, Brentwood does not need to hand them an automatic third vote.
That means Districts 2 and 4 matter enormously.
District 2: Tammy Zickuhr
Let’s start with Kris Brand.
I think it’s safe to say he’s Not the Brand Brentwood Needs.
His audition for City Council while serving on the Planning Commission has been an utter failure.
Fire stations. Restaurants. Business issues. Development decisions. Pick a topic and somehow we end up with another unnecessary controversy.
Worse, Brand has already said he would vote on the Planning Commission according to his conscience rather than the law.
Excuse me?
That’s not how a Planning Commission works.
You’re not sitting around deciding whether pineapple belongs on pizza. There are laws, ordinances, findings and property rights involved — or in the case of fire stations, gyms and sleeping quarters.
The moment a planning commissioner says personal conscience comes before the law, somebody should be checking whether his microphone is still plugged in.
Then add the likelihood of Brand becoming the third vote for Meyer and Mendoza.
Hard pass.
Vinoy Mereddy is a much different story.
He’s smart, enthusiastic and has put in the work throughout the community. I like a lot of what he brings to the table.
But right now, I actually think Brentwood could benefit more from Mereddy serving on the Planning Commission.
God knows that commission could use some help.
That brings me to Tammy Zickuhr.
I absolutely adore Zickuhr as a human being, but that’s not why I’m supporting her.
She has faults. She’ll tell you she has faults. She doesn’t pretend to know everything.
Good.
Give me somebody willing to say, “I don’t know, let me figure it out,” over somebody walking into City Hall believing they descended from Mount Diablo carrying the Ten Commandments of municipal government.
Zickuhr will come into council meetings open-minded. She isn’t arriving with every vote predetermined.
More importantly, because of her own experiences dealing with government, she understands what happens when bureaucracy forgets there are actual human beings and businesses on the other side of the counter.
She has regional relationships, understands the county and isn’t obsessed with creating another layer of government oversight every time somebody sneezes.
Brentwood could use a little less government knows best and a little more common sense.
District 4: Holley Bishop-Mello
For District 4, my choice is Holley Bishop-Mello.
She may actually be the most misunderstood candidate in this election.
But she is also battle-tested.
She knows Brentwood. She knows the business community. She’ll stand up for residents, and she’s not afraid to push back when something doesn’t make sense.
And right now, City Hall desperately needs people willing to ask the incredibly complicated governmental question: “Why the hell are we doing this?”
Sometimes that’s all it takes.
Bishop-Mello isn’t going to sit quietly while ridiculous policy sails through because questioning it might make somebody uncomfortable.
Good.
Government could use a little discomfort.
Stephanie Williams-Rogers seems like a perfectly nice person.
But her record gives me serious concerns.
During her time on the Brentwood Union School District Board, I didn’t see the kind of outspoken advocacy for parents I would expect from somebody now asking voters to put her on the City Council.
Then there’s the Toro Viejo Taqueria mess.
Williams-Rogers, who has a child at Kiddie Academy, wrote a letter expressing disappointment after the Planning Commission approved the restaurant’s CUP. That helped put the issue back into the political arena when Vice Mayor Meyer pulled the item for council review on the final day it could be requested.
The end result?
A taqueria gets treated differently regarding alcohol than the Chinese restaurant next door.
Because apparently tequila requires a municipal emergency while a mai tai is just Tuesday.
This is exactly the kind of nonsense businesses shouldn’t have to navigate.
Sheree Shannon is another candidate I enjoyed interviewing. She has experience and, like Mereddy, could be an asset on the Planning Commission.
But for City Council, I need more.
“I decided to run after a friend called and suggested it” may explain how the journey started, but it can’t be the vision.
Why are you running?
What specifically needs fixing?
Where should Brentwood be in four years?
That’s the job.
And right now, Bishop-Mello has convinced me she’s better prepared for it.
District 4: Holley Bishop-Mello.
Brentwood Needs a Two-Year Pause
Electing Bishop-Mello and Zickuhr would do something Brentwood desperately needs: Restore balance.
This isn’t an argument for two years of political gridlock.
It’s an argument for two years of somebody occasionally hitting the brakes before City Hall drives the car through another wall and schedules a workshop six months later to discuss why there’s a giant hole in the building.
Policies should be challenged.
Consequences should be considered before votes happen.
Businesses shouldn’t have to hire a fortune teller to determine how City Hall will interpret the rules this week.
Residents shouldn’t have to worry that criticizing elected officials will result in an online mob showing up at their digital doorstep.
And councilmembers shouldn’t be building political armies. They should be building relationships.
Bishop-Mello and Zickuhr understand businesses need consistency to compete and succeed. They understand public safety matters. They understand regional relationships matter. And, perhaps most importantly, neither appears interested in becoming an automatic third vote for anyone.
That’s exactly what Brentwood needs.
The city doesn’t need more bridges burned.
It doesn’t need more political warfare.
It doesn’t need more trolls, deepfakes, personal vendettas or government-by-Facebook-comment-section.
It needs adults willing to disagree, ask questions, work together and occasionally admit somebody else might have a point.
Brentwood doesn’t need to be reinvented.
It needs to remember what made it successful in the first place.
Better in Brentwood.
Remember that?
Maybe it’s time City Hall did, too.
This election should be about putting out fires — not electing more people to pour gasoline on them.
For District 2, my vote is Tammy Zickuhr.
For District 4, my vote is Holley Bishop-Mello.
Give Brentwood two years to cool down, clean up the mess and get back to being Brentwood.
[Mike Burkholder]
