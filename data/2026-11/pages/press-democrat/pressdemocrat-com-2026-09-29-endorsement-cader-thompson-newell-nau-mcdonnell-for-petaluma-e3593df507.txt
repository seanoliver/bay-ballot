Endorsement: Cader Thompson, Newell, Nau, McDonnell for Petaluma City Council

Petaluma’s politics are about as divided as party allegiances in the United States.
Residents are either happy with the status quo … or they abhor it.
Some say Petaluma’s council is too aggressive with new bike lanes, while others praise efforts to increase mobility while reducing the city’s carbon footprint.
One side blasts the council’s spending habits, while the other commends much-needed improvements.
And then there’s the downtown luxury hotel — which, after four years of public outcry, developer delays and heated discussions, officially died in early September when the developer announced it was walking away from the deal.
There are reasons to be frustrated. The city faces an ongoing budget deficit, even after making $1.6 million in cuts this year. The council has also been under fire over how it handled the scrutiny and aftermath of the North Bay Animal Services fiasco.
But there is also plenty to celebrate. The Petaluma Swim Center, Lucchesi Park and the Senior Center parking lot have all been upgraded, as have many miles of public roadway with more to come. And soon, crews will break ground on a $70 million public safety building at the Petaluma Fairgrounds to house both the police and fire departments.
As a result of this push-and-pull, voters have many candidates from which to choose. Here are The Press Democrat’s recommendations for the four open seats.

District 1
Of the three candidates running for the District 1 seat, The Press Democrat recommends the incumbent, Janice Cader Thompson. While Cader Thompson can — at times — be a loose cannon at the dais, there’s no doubt that she is a fighter for her constituents. Cader Thompson represents residents of Petaluma’s mobile home parks, and not only does she knock on doors, she knows her constituents by name and ensures they aren’t getting left behind.
On the other hand, one of her competitors, Alan LaPierre, is the property manager of Petaluma Estates Mobile Home Park while his wife, Beth LaPierre, manages Petaluma’s Youngstown Mobile Home Park, which is currently trying to raise rates on the tenants Cader Thompson works tirelessly to protect.
Another challenger, Nate Martin, a general manager at the local Staples store, said he is concerned about the direction the city is headed, but he lacks experience or clear direction for where he wants to take it.
The Press Democrat endorses Janice Cader Thompson.
District 2
The race for District 2 pits one environmentalist with council experience — John Shribbs — against another without it — Brent Newell. Shribbs, a retired biological research scientist and high school science teacher, is the incumbent. He mostly votes in the city’s best interest, but can waste time on the dais. This appears to frustrate some of his colleagues, which is perhaps why fellow council members Brian Barnacle, Kevin McDonnell, Janice Cader Thompson, Karen Nau and Frank Quint have all endorsed Newell, an environmental lawyer instead.
The Press Democrat likes Newell’s fresh attitude, and his efforts and push to meet as many community members as possible have not gone unnoticed, as he has connected with groups throughout the district. The only pause Newell gives us is over his priorities; while his top priority is to bring more affordable housing to the city, which is great, his next priorities “are those you identify because I will work with you to achieve your goals,” his website reads.
Petaluma needs a leader who has strong policymaking skills. We want to give Newell the benefit of the doubt that the work he’s putting in now will continue to guide his policymaking. The Press Democrat recommends a vote for Brent Newell.
District 3
The race for District 3 is surprisingly competitive, pitting well-known incumbent Karen Nau against newcomer Eric Vazquez. Nau, running for her second consecutive term, has served as a good representative for Petaluma’s rural residents and provides a more conservative balance to the council’s progressive stance. She has been a workhorse for the city and her district, improving city parks, stopping illegal dumping at the American Little League Park, funding street repairs in front of Miwok Elementary School and more.
Nau is endorsed by the city’s firefighter and police associations, as well as the Northern California Engineering Contractors Association.
Vazquez, who was born in Petaluma to immigrants from Honduras and Mexico, says he understands what it means to have to make ends meet, adding that “government should be built around the needs of working people, not around whoever has the loudest voice or the deepest pockets.” His endorsements run deep and include State Assemblymembers Chris Rogers and Damon Connolly, the North Bay Labor Council, state and local Democratic parties, Teamsters 665, Latino PAC of Sonoma County and a handful of former Petaluma City Council members.
Vazquez would bring a fresh outlook and perspective to the council that hasn’t always been there. His experience includes working as an affordable housing policymaker at the local and state levels, and his background serving on the Petaluma Transit Advisory Committee and boards of the Petaluma Museum Association and Sonoma County Conservation Action has given him an introduction to city politics, too.
Vazquez’s voice matters, and we hope to see more of him in city politics. But ultimately Nau’s experience, kindness and keen ability to serve as a connection between the city’s different factions is vital for Petaluma residents right now. The Press Democrat recommends a vote for Karen Nau.
Mayor
Three candidates are vying for the mayor’s seat, including incumbent Kevin McDonnell, former supervisor candidate Shelina Moreda and small businessman John Hanania.
Hanania, a fixture at Petaluma City Council meetings, last ran for council in 2022 in a three-way race in District 3, and lost with 31.5% of the vote. Last year he reentered local politics by spearheading an attempt to recall Cader Thompson, which failed to obtain enough public support to go to a ballot.
Moreda, a wildcard in this election, appears to be running as an alternative to the status quo — which is appealing to the city’s critics, many of whom appear to be very vocal one-issue voters with a particular ax to grind, such as animal services or the downtown hotel. But Moreda, who has no real experience as an elected leader or policymaker, offers nothing herself other than not being “the establishment.” In addition, her divisive campaign style, and notable lack of transparency, indicates to us that she is not qualified.
For his part, McDonnell has served on the council since 2019. At that time, the city faced a $4.7 million deficit, driven by losses in sales, hotel and property tax revenue. In part due to McDonnell’s leadership, the city climbed out of the COVID-19 pandemic’s fiscal crisis, which many neighboring cities failed to do, and now appears to be on a road to recovery while looking to navigate a future made uncertain by larger economic forces..
McDonnell’s leadership and expertise managing budget crises, as well as his ability to speak to all Petalumans, will be a boon as the city grapples with that uncertainty. The Press Democrat recommends a vote for Kevin McDonnell.
