Endorsement: No on Prop 4, don’t let politicians raid public funds for their campaigns

In the run-up to the June primaries for the Sonoma County Fourth District Supervisor race, the three candidates vying to make it to the November showdown collectively raised more than $631,280 in campaign cash.
But nearly 40% of that money in the accounts of independent committees that campaigned on behalf of a candidate.
Cloverdale Vice Mayor Todd Lands — who didn’t move forward in the primaries — benefited from a $150,000 independent expenditure committee established by Molly Flater. Tom Schwedhelm, who finished second in the primaries, was supported by a multi-faceted political action committee that spent more than $51,000. And nearly $47,000 in an SEIU Local 1021 independent committee gave a boon to Melanie Bagby, who won the primaries.
This kind of spending, not just in Sonoma County, but throughout the state, where organizations sometimes pour millions of dollars into a campaign, has pushed legislators to ask voters this November to repeal a 1988 law and allow taxpayer dollars to be spent on campaigns.
A yes vote on California Prop. 4 would allow public officers and candidates to accept public funds — taxpayer dollars — for campaign purposes. A yes vote would allow state and local governments to create programs so that public funds — again, taxpayer dollars — could be set aside for election campaigns.
The Press Democrat recommends voting no on Prop. 4. Our tax dollars should not be spent on election campaigns.
Many Sonoma and Napa county cities are already struggling financially. Three Sonoma County and two Napa County cities have sales tax measure on the ballot this year in an effort to bring more revenue into their general funds. In 2024, three cities, including Cloverdale, Sebastopol and Sonoma, placed sales tax measures on the ballot. Some of those cities still face a budget deficit and for others that might be sitting on the fence, there is no wiggle room in their budgets to create a fund for campaign financing.
Moreover, people should be able to decide whether or not they want their money to go toward supporting a candidate. Our elected officials should not make that decision for us — especially if they are up for re-election.
We understand the desire to take corporate money out of elections. This year’s race for California governor highlights that need more than ever. A political spending committee comprising the state realtors association, California Chamber of Commerce, Pacific Gas & Electric and the state electrical workers union spent a record $32 million on ads opposing Democratic billionaire Tom Steyer, who ultimately placed third in the primary.
Leading into those primaries, Democratic nominee Xavier Becerra — whose name appears on the November ballot — was backed by Meta, AirBnB and Uber at $1 million each and Chevron, McDonalds and DaVita at $500,000 each, to name a few.
Those corporate dollars force the fair question: If elected, how confident are we that Becerra will have the people’s interests at heart, and not those corporations?
We agree that corporations should not be able to buy candidates or campaigns, but Prop. 4 is not the answer. Creating a fund of taxpayer dollars and making it available for election campaigns is not the answer. Those hard-earned dollars belong in a municipality’s general fund, to be used on improving roadways, funding for public safety officers or enhancing our local parks.
The measure has been endorsed by organizations with which we normally agree, including the California Democratic Party, California Working Families Party, League of Women Voters of California, ACLU of California and California Common Cause.
But this time, we agree with the California Taxpayers Association, which opposes the measure.
David Kline, Vice President of Communications and Research for California Taxpayers Association, said it best: “People when they pay taxes want to pay for schools, fire departments, libraries, roads, that sort of thing. The last thing they want to pay for is a negative political ad that is going to hit them on the radio or TV during election season.”
The Press Democrat recommends a No vote on California Prop. 4.
