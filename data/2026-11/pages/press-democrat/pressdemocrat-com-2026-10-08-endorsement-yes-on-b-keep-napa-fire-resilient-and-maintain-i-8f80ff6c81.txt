Nine years ago, a new breed of destructive wildfire clusters ripped through parts of Napa County. The Tubbs, Nuns and Atlas fires of , which burned more than 80,000 acres, still live rent-free in the minds of many Napans.
“Almost everyone in Napa — whether a city dweller or living in south county — has some personal connection to or was impacted by the fires,” Joe Nordlinger, the chief executive officer of Napa Communities FireWise Foundation told The Press Democrat Editorial Board.
Since then, Nordlinger and his team have worked tirelessly to prepare the community for the next big fire — of which there have been four major and a handful of minor conflagrations.
But they haven’t been as bad as 2017, thanks in part to Nordlinger’s efforts. Under his leadership, the county’s fire safe councils network grew from seven to 26. From those councils, Napa FireWise formed a countywide CWPP, or Community Wildfire Protection Plan, creating a centralized platform to help eliminate fragmentation among entities focused on wildfire resilience.
They could do more, but funding is an issue.
That’s why he, along with Melanie Parker, chief executive officer of the Land Trust of Napa County, are asking Napa voters for help.
Measure B is a proposed new half-cent sales tax that would bring $23 million annually into the county for wildfire prevention and open space preservation maintenance. As a citizens’ initiative sales tax, it needs a simple majority to pass. The sales tax would go into effect on and end in 2045.
Half of the revenue would go toward grants for fire prevention and resiliency projects. The other half would fund the Napa County Regional Park and Open Space District’s efforts to protect and preserve Napa’s open spaces.
Why are the two forces joining up?
Teaming with Napa FireWise bridges the gap between ecological land management and fire safety expertise. As Parker said, wildfire resilience and conservation cannot be achieved in isolation. Protecting watersheds, managing large parks, restoring overgrown open spaces and maintaining buffer zones around towns are directly linked to reducing fire risks for adjacent communities.
Such work is vital to the continued protection of Napa’s residents and lands from the next big fire. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote for Napa County’s Measure B.
Today’s conservation practices have shifted from strictly preserving land to active management. But active land management plans — from fuel reduction projects, forest thinning and controlled grazing — require funding that neither Napa FireWise or the open space district has. Both organizations have adept grant writers and have been able to manage a lot with county funding. For example, Napa FireWise has crossed off 347 or nearly 600 fire mitigation projects since 2017. But those projects require continued maintenance — and maintenance requires continued funding.
The open space district has been criticized by the Napa County Farm Bureau for failing to consistently maintain its land, in some cases putting “agricultural land at greater risk for fire and pest issues.” Parker said a consistent funding source would empower the open space district to perform a higher level of active land stewardship the Farm Bureau desires and lands deserve.
Such sales tax efforts, brought forth by the county, have failed in the past. A previous county-sponsored attempt to pass a quarter-cent sales tax measure for fire maintenance work — the 2022 Measure L — fell short of a needed two-thirds majority, as did two previous attempts to pass quarter-cent tax measures for open space work in 2020 and 2017. But each of those failed measures received well over 50% of the vote.
As climate change continues to make North Bay summers hotter and dryer, this new funding source could help bring continued peace of mind to Napa County residents. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote on Measure B.
