With at least two incumbents leaving their posts and one candidate running unopposed, the 2026 election will bring Windsor at least two new council members.
The change could be a welcome shake-up for many Windsor residents, who have been frustrated with town leaders over the last few years.
Town criers say the council has approved too many affordable housing developments, restricting the town’s property tax potential while crowding roadways and increasing the use of available water.
Moreover, with Windsor at a fiscal crossroads, leaders have been forced to make decisions some residents have deemed unpopular: They cut hours at the senior pool and funding to the town’s nonprofits.
But they’ve also reduced expenses by 10% in each department and dipped into reserves to backfill its budgetary gap.
For some residents, the last straw was the council’s ask of voters to approve a one-cent sales tax to boost the town’s coffers. District 1 Council member Mike Wall — who is not seeking reelection — said he would support the council putting the sales tax before the voters, but would not vote for the sales tax himself.

Two strong candidates are contending for Wall’s position. As current Planning Commissioners, Evan Kubota and Gina Fortino Dickson carry intimate knowledge of the inner workings of town hall and a strong understanding of Windsor’s general plan. They share the same concern for the town’s ongoing fiscal crisis and say the budget requires oversight and a potential overhaul.
Kubota, a security executive endorsed by Wall, told The Press Democrat he recognized the structural budget deficit must be dealt with “to ensure the financial health of the town is prioritized.” Kubota noted that potential funds from the town’s proposed one-cent sales tax would help bridge the deficit, but also says the town needs to attract more businesses — and more revenue — to the area.
Fortino Dickson, who lost the District 1 seat to Wall in 2022, told The Press Democrat that Measure I is only the beginning of what she sees is a “two-part process to improve the Town’s long-term fiscal outlook, given the rise in providing public safety services and investing in our parks and infrastructure.” Her multifaceted approach to the town’s fiscal challenges would also include seeking grant funding and streamlining the budget to avoid duplicating processes.
Both candidates have a keen focus on the town’s growth. Like many Windsor residents, both Kubota and Fortino Dickson say the town has approved its fair share of affordable housing projects required by the state.
Kubota said now is the time to “take steps to prioritize local control over development projects,” which he would do by updating the town’s zoning and development standards.
“Growth is inescapable, but thoughtful growth has become a challenge,” Kubota said.
Fortino Dickson said she understands that many people in Windsor — including her two children — are either unable to pay high rents or afford middle-income, first-time home purchases. Her aim is to change that by focusing on bringing in more “housing for the missing middle and small starter homes and ADU’s for those seeking to achieve the dream of homeownership.”
District 1 — and the town in general — will be fortunate to have either candidate serving on the council. But at this critical juncture of the financial crisis, the town needs an experienced council member with deep government knowledge and experience who can quickly dive into policy-making decisions beyond community growth, land use, and development. Voters can find that in Gina Fortino Dickson.
Fortino Dickson previously served on the Clearlake City Council before moving to Windsor . Upon her arrival, she worked for the Sonoma County Office of Education, where she managed a $15 million grant to support youth and educational programs. She was also part of the Sonoma County Department of Emergency Management before becoming an attorney and has served on the town’s Parks and Recreation Commission, on the Sonoma County Child Care Planning Council and as a commissioner on the Sonoma County Commission for the Status of Women.
It is clear Fortino Dickson has substantial municipal government experience that would benefit the town as it continues to manage its fiscal crisis.
The Press Democrat endorses Gina Fortino Dickson for Windsor Town Council District 1.

Mayor Rosa Reynoza is not seeking reelection.
In her stead, current Vice Mayor Tanya Potter — the current representative for District 4 — and Rick Massell are vying for the two-year role.
Massell, a retired educator, said he’s running to provide a contest between candidates, ensuring residents have an opportunity to hear strong civic discussion about the issues facing Windsor.
Both candidates are focused on closing the structural deficit in the town’s general fund.
Potter told The Press Democrat she would focus on economic development to bring in consistent revenue sources for the town. She also noted she’d like to bring the wastewater facility to completion. The completion of this project will turn biosolids into urban biochar, which could save the town $1 million annually and serve as a revenue source.
Massell, in the Sonoma County League of Women Voters forum, said he’d like town staff and leaders to find “unique” funding opportunities, and should turn to staff and residents for suggestions on what other services could be cut.
Potter has a comprehensive knowledge of the town’s struggles and goals. She is meticulous and analytical in her decisions and policy-making. The Press Democrat endorses Tanya Potter for Windsor’s mayor.
