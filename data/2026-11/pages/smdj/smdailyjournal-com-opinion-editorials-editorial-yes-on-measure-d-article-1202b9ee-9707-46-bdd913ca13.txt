Editorial: Yes on Measure D
Raising the hotel tax in Burlingame from 12% to 15% to generate an additional $5.7 million a year for the city’s coffers is a no-brainer.
Out-of-town visitors bring revenue but they also bring impact, and the idea behind the tax is that the revenue will offset that impact on our roads, parks and other infrastructure. Before COVID, the city collected approximately $30 million in revenue from the 12% hotel tax. In its last budget, the city showed it collected approximately $23 million.
Before COVID, the hotel tax provided about 35% of the city’s revenue and the most recent budget showed it provided about 23% of the city’s $93 million annual general fund budget. Hotel tax revenue is one of the more consistent drivers of tax revenue in Burlingame, and its 12% rate is lower than most other cities on the Peninsula. Bringing it up to just under the peak rate for all cities (Menlo Park has a 15.5% rate) will keep the city’s revenue stream strong.
One could argue that keeping the lower rate would provide an incentive for visitors to stay in Burlingame over other cities but it would represent an increase of about $6 for an average night’s stay of around $200. The convenience of staying in Burlingame because of its proximity to the airport and the beauty of the city’s location would likely not be a deterrent at this rate.
This is a modest increase in a tax paid by visitors who do spend money here and provide sales tax but who don’t otherwise contribute to our tax base. It will help smooth out the city’s finances from an industry that hasn’t quite recovered from COVID and may also be affected by diminished business travel yet won’t have a large impact on drawing visitors. This is a good financial move for the city, and Measure D deserves your support.
