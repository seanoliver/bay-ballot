San Bruno voters will be able to change the city’s business tax model into a tiered system that will make it more fair for small businesses, with a carveout for those who provide affordable housing.
The current system is regressive, meaning the more money a business makes, the less it will pay. The shift will allow the city to charge differing rates per thousands of dollars in income for different types of businesses. If approved by a majority of voters on the upcoming November ballot, the new business license fee system could generate San Bruno an additional $4.2 million in revenue annually, to be spent on essential city services like 911 response, fixing streets and maintaining parks, according to the ballot measure.
The new measure proposes a $75 flat fee for up to $75,000 in revenue, plus an additional $1 per $1,000 in income for general retail and administrative businesses, $1.50 per $1,000 for service-based shops, $2 for contractors, professional and advanced businesses, and $3 per $1,000 for property rental companies.
The tax is not designed to target small businesses, and more than 75% of businesses will not see a change if it’s approved. This means the city will see more revenue and smaller businesses will not see an increase. The new business license fees on property rental companies will also not apply to affordable housing providers, per state law, and those renting out an accessory dwelling unit or room from their primary residence will be exempted.
These are small changes, but important ones for a city contending with state funding takeaways. It also makes the tax more fair for small businesses who were paying more than their fair share. Vote yes.
