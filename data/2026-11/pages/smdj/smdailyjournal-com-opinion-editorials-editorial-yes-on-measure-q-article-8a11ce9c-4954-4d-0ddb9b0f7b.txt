Editorial: Yes on Measure Q
A long and contentious battle over a proposed five-story building in downtown Half Moon Bay will reach a tipping point this election as voters will decide whether they want to open up a larger battle with the state.
While the measure is focused on the affordable housing development for senior farmworkers at 555 Kelly Ave., and whether it should be allowed, it will also either quell a disagreement with the state or enlarge it.
Those advocating for 555 Kelly and a yes vote on Measure Q see the 40-unit development as an obvious way to provide for one of Half Moon Bay’s most vulnerable populations while meeting state-mandated requirements on affordable housing.
All other potential solutions are in the ether and most of the concerns are red herrings, and the city should comply with regulations as set forward by the state. We believe the building would be better suited for the city if it were not as tall, but it is not a tower.
This is the development that was proposed, and the one that was approved by the City Council.
It will be properly managed and eventually accepted. Not only that, it provides a form of housing that is needed by the community. It provides housing described as needed in the city’s housing element. It should be built.
Measure Q deserves a yes vote.
