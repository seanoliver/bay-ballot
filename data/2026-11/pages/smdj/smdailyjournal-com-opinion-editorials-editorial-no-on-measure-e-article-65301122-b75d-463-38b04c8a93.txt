Editorial: No on Measure E
The grassroots effort to propose stronger rent control and tenant protections in Redwood City has tapped into a sentiment — that rising housing costs are a problem in making sure it remains a diverse and welcoming city.
However, the approach the proponents took may also cause more harm in good. Because Measure E does not provide per-unit fee exemptions for below-market rate housing providers, it may actually deter such nonprofits from providing that type of housing in the city. This is a real miss, a substantial miss, and a fatal flaw in the measure.
This has been the issue with ballot measures for rent control in local cities. Rather than work through the process of the city council, a measure is placed on the ballot through signature gathering. For current renters, ensuring costs don’t rise makes financial sense, and putting pen to paper on a petition isn’t hard. The caps apply to approximately 19% of the city’s housing stock because of the vagaries of California law, and the cap in this measure is 5% for qualified units built before 1995 versus the state’s allowance of up to 10%. However, all rental units would be subject to the tenant protections outlined in the measure.
If Measure E passes, landlords would be required to pay hefty relocation assistance for any and all no-fault just-cause terminations of leases that equates to four months of fair market rent or a $12,000 minimum, whichever is higher. Examples of these qualified terminations of leases include substantial remodeling, owner move in or withdrawal of the property from the rental market.
A tenant’s right to return would also be expanded under Measure E. Should a property owner demolish a building and then rebuild on the same property within five years of a terminated lease, the tenant has the right to return at the same rental rate, despite the significant remodel.
These measures are very specific and likely wouldn’t benefit many renters overall, but it would give a chill to anyone thinking of providing housing in the city as there is a risk.
Proponents of the measure have forged ahead because of what they see as a real need, and they believe measures the city has taken are not enough. But this measure, as written, is the wrong approach and could further hinder the real efforts officials are making to address this issue. Vote no.
