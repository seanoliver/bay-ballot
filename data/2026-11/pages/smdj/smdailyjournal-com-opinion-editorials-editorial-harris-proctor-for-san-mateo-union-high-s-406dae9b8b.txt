Editorial: Harris, Proctor for San Mateo Union High School District board
There are three open seats on the San Mateo Union High School District Board of Trustees with incumbent Teri Chavez running unopposed. The other two seats are contested. One seat has long been held by Trustee Robert Griffin, who opted out of reelection. Well deserved, and we owe Griffin a large amount of gratitude for filling the role through both good times and bad, with both easy and difficult decisions.
Running for his Area 5 seat is Alison Proctor, who is finishing her second term on the San Mateo-Foster City School District board. That district is the largest in the county at 10,500 students, and has had its share of both good times and bad, with both easy and difficult decisions. Proctor has long been known as an active listener who digs into data and makes decisions based on what is best for students first, but also considers the impact on the larger school community. Her main approach is through collaboration while ensuring students have what it takes to succeed. In a district as large as SM-FC, it’s not always an easy task. We have seen the district through various board members and superintendents, and this current administration is capable, professional and high functioning. Much of that can be attributed to a leader such as Proctor, who will continue doing good things as a member of the San Mateo Union High School District Board of Trustees. Her opponent, Kimo Rosenbaum, is bright and passionate, but the position is better suited for someone of Proctor’s caliber.
For the Area 3 seat, there are two candidates who have a firm grasp on the district’s issues. Incumbent Jennifer Jacobson is taking on challenger Diana Harris. Both are well-researched and on top of the district’s issues.
Yet Harris would be a better fit for the position. As a teacher in the San Mateo-Foster City School District, she knows what the current classroom challenges are for those at the ground level in the education field. The San Mateo Union High School District has benefited from having Greg Land, a middle school principal, on its board as that perspective lends a certain amount of expertise. Having a teacher on the board, albeit from a different district, can only help round out the perspective. Some may think a teacher only brings a limited perspective, but that is unfair as everyone knows people can wear different hats in their lives. Besides, Harris is also an active parent of a child in the high school district and is well versed in explaining perspectives to various groups. We also like her idea of having regular community listening sessions outside of board meetings to reach those who may be unable to attend.
Jacobson appears to have learned a lot in her time on the board, and likely would have benefited from holding such sessions to make sure she was representing the viewpoint of the community at large and not just a confined constituency when it comes to important issues — particularly ones that can spark controversy if not handled in a respectful and informed way. While it is the role of an elected officials to ask questions of administrators and staff on policy, we have been disappointed in how she has gone about that task when it comes to topics such as ethnic studies or gender dysphoria. Jacobson has evolved on certain topics after holding discussions with district families, and we believe that is a positive. Personal growth is good, but a board trustee’s job is to listen first, then ask questions, and do so in a way that is productive for all. We don’t think this has always been the case with Jacobson.
We believe she would be best suited to be an active and informed participant from the audience rather than on the board.
Instead, we believe Harris would hit the ground running. She is knowledgable, informed and a natural connector and collaborator. She deserves your vote.
