Editorial: Nash, Newsom, Sahae for San Mateo City Council
The members of the San Mateo City Council work hard, do their homework, collect various viewpoints and navigate challenging topics in mostly a collegial and productive way — all while handling the wild bag of cats that is the various interested and active political forces in the city.
The city of San Mateo is facing financial challenges, not because of mismanagement, but rather a set of circumstances beyond its control. The state taking away vehicle license fee money combined with lower property tax revenue brought on by slow property sales swamped by high interest rates is causing a deficit. City officials have been active on this issue and have presented an option to voters to ensure services remain essentially the same. If it fails, officials are prepped to make the choices necessary to keep the city solvent while the VLF resolves and tax revenue returns. This may not be everyone’s preferred choice, but it is a responsible approach.
When it comes to other city issues, the members of the City Council are trying to find their way and are legitimately doing so in a thoughtful and earnest manner. No one on this current City Council is avoiding hot topics, concerned residents or difficult challenges.
In District 1, Lisa Diaz Nash is ubiquitous and earnest, always seeking a win for all or at least a win for most. There are councilmembers who thrive on retail politics, the “what can I do for you?” type and those who thrive on taking on big challenges. Nash does both. We sincerely wish the process for the historic ordinance update could have gone better, but it was a challenging situation made more difficult by the personality conflicts involved. Nash did her best. With the proposal for the Horizon treatment center, Nash was the first elected official who pushed for more communication and transparency. She is a gifted politician, whose enthusiasm for helping people is admirable. Her touch on big issues will only get more deft.
In District 3, Rob Newsom doesn’t share Nash’s speed dial mentality but can get things done in his own right and illustrates a strong sense of ensuring everyone’s needs are met. In his work on the Delaware Street bicycle lane proposal, he showed that meeting people where they are, listening, compromising and coming up with creative solutions can stem controversy and produce wins. This is a mark of a true leader. Rather than using information to advance an agenda, Newsom uses it make sure more people are being served and can feel good about the direction of the city. Progress is made in the middle, and this is where Newsom shines. He understands that policy should be adapted to people rather than people adapted to policy. There is a big difference between activism and civil service, and Newsom’s heart is in the latter where a councilmember needs to operate and finds the most success.
In District 5, incumbent Adam Loraine is a kind and thoughtful councilmember who has made his positions clear while trying to fold in alternative points of view when possible. He is responsive and responsible and, overall, the city has been well served by his tenure. However, there comes candidates at times who outshine them all, and that is clearly Dana Yates Sahae. In the interest of full disclosure, a Daily Journal assistant editor has contributed to her campaign, and they are friends. But that editor has no role in deciding our political endorsements. Sahae also worked at the Daily Journal in the early 2000s as a reporter. So we know her. But just as potential employers will ask previous employers for a reference, we can be honest about our experience with her and discuss her potential as a councilmember through the recent endorsement process.

What we can say is that there is no one better suited for this position. She has strong ideas, and knows the city and its issues. She grew up around its politics and is a quick learner, deep thinker and one of the hardest working people around. Her father Gary Yates was one of the best councilmembers this city has ever seen and has had a great impact on San Mateo. He was the epitome of responsibility and responsiveness and had civic duty in his DNA. So does Sahae.
She knows the city, but also the district and is willing to work to make sure its residents are heard and, most importantly, get what they need. And what you see is what you get. If she doesn’t know something, she will find out. If you have a good idea, she will run with it.
The current City Council has been challenged at times by its lack of institutional knowledge. Sahae can bring that knowledge, along with a fresh approach to issues that have long plagued the city.
This could be a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity to bring someone of her ability on board. If we do, this city will only benefit. It is with great enthusiasm that we endorse Sahae for this position.
San Mateo has had its recent challenges, but its council continually rises to meet these challenges and their growing experience will only help. Nash and Newsom, along with Sahae, will truly bring the council and the city to the next level through their measured and practical approaches, sound management, responsiveness and responsibility to San Mateo and its people.
