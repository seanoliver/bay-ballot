2026 East Bay DSA Voter Guide
Welcome to the East Bay DSA Voter Guide!
East Bay DSA has prepared the following analysis of ballot measures on the basis of our chapter’s Electoral Platform and DSA’s National Program. For information on state-wide ballot measures, please consult California DSA's voter guide.
How Do I Vote?
You can visit the California Secretary of State’s website to find your nearest polling location. Same day voter registration is available at county election offices and polling locations.
To vote by mail drop box, visit your county's website for your nearest location: Alameda County Contra Costa County Solano County Napa County
Ongoing efforts to restrict voting access and delay mail service may impact your ability to vote. In line with the Secretary of State’s guidance, if you are voting by mail, we strongly recommend completing and returning your ballot as soon as you receive it.
For information on your rights as a voter, we recommend referring to the ACLU of Northern California’s Know Your Rights Information.
Endorsements vs. Recommendations
Endorsements have been approved by our membership and entail a significant commitment of chapter resources, time, and energy. These are candidates and measures that advance our socialist demands. Endorsed measures and candidates are indicated with a 🌹.
Recommendations are not endorsements but are still valuable measures that can build working class power. Analysis is informed by our East Bay Electoral Platform and DSA's national program, “Workers Deserve More!”
For measures where we take No Position, we provide insight into how that measure could potentially benefit or hurt working class interests.
Analysis of statewide ballot measures and races is provided by California DSA.
East Bay DSA 2026 Electoral Platform
The East Bay DSA 2026 Electoral Platform is a core set of demands that are meant to unite our campaigns and committees in fighting for the working class. Its planks represent reforms that we can push for here and now as we organize to win our program for democratic socialist transformation. When our candidates enter office, the platform provides the basis on which we support their work and connect it to our organizing.

Measure I - School Parcel Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure I.
Measure I renews the expiring $96 school parcel tax, plus an additional $48 per parcel, for a total tax of $144 per parcel. This measure requires a ⅔ majority YES to pass. If approved, this parcel tax replaces Measure E, a parcel tax passed in 2019, which will expire on . If Measure I is not passed, Measure E will remain in effect until , at which point it will expire. Measure I is projected to raise approximately $2,900,000 ($2.9 million) annually for nine (9) years. It includes an exemption for senior-citizen homeowners, which is unfortunately not limited to lower-income residents. It also includes exemptions for people on SSI/SSDI (Supplemental Security Income/Social Security Disability Insurance), as well as independent oversight, a ban on using Measure I money to fund administrators’ salaries, and solely local control of funds. Measure I represents the first parcel tax increase since 2008.
Measure I seeks to provide local funding for Dublin schools that cannot be taken by the state, is locally controlled, and is subject to independent oversight. The goals of Measure I are to maintain small class sizes, attract and retain quality teachers, as well as provide enhanced STEM, reading, writing, visual and performing arts programs for students. A reduction in class sizes and an increase in teacher salaries and benefits were two important wins from the Dublin Teachers’ Association Strike in . The official Ballot Measure Submittal Form includes an argument in favor of the measure submitted by DUSD teachers, parents, a school principal, the Chair of the DUSD Parcel Tax Oversight Committee, and notable local officials Tim Sbranti, the former mayor of Dublin, and Kashef Qaadri, a current Dublin City Councilmember.
This is the first parcel tax increase in Dublin since 2008. For the past six years, we’ve seen how a global pandemic, unfettered crony capitalism, and rampant federal funding cuts negatively impacts students’ education. This measure will invest in the youngest members of our society, honor DTA’s contract, and allow DUSD to avoid (or delay) more budget cuts.
Measure J - Education Parcel Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure J.
Measure J renews the existing $99 parcel tax for twelve (12) years, generating approximately $1.8 million annually. This measure requires a ⅔ majority YES to pass. If approved, this parcel tax would go into effect on . If Measure J is not passed, San Lorenzo’s current education parcel tax will expire on . The measure includes exemptions for seniors 65+ and people on SSI/SSDI (Supplemental Security Income/Social Security Disability Insurance), as well as multiple accountability provisions. These accountability provisions require the District to create a separate account for parcel tax funds, produce an annual financial report for, and submit to an annual audit by, the District's governing board, and be subject to review by an appointed citizen oversight committee to ensure that the funds are properly used for the purposes listed in the measure.
Measure J seeks to renew San Lorenzo’s existing education parcel tax to provide local funding for SLUSD schools that cannot be taken by the state, is locally controlled, and is subject to independent oversight. The goals of Measure J are to support student academic programs, prepare students for college and the workplace, and to attract and retain highly qualified teachers and staff, especially in math, science, and special education.
Measure J seeks to renew an already-existing parcel tax.
Measure K - School Bond
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure K.
Measure K allows the Sunol Glen Unified School District to issue $6.1 million in bonds (financed by property tax increases) to fund repairs of the Main Building, replace aging plumbing, electrical, and heating systems, and upgrade classrooms and restrooms. This measure requires a 55% YES vote to pass. If passed, there will be an annual tax on the assessed value of a taxed property, with an average of $33.54 per $100,000 of assessed property value per year. This bond is estimated to generate $431,700 annually while the bonds are outstanding. The final fiscal year in which this tax will be collected is 2058-2059. The authorization for this tax includes provisions for annual independent financial and performance audits, oversight by the Independent Citizens' Oversight Committee, and a prohibition on using the Bond money to pay for teacher or administrator salaries.
Sunol Glen School is a K-8 school and the only school in the Sunol Glen Unified School District. Per the 2020 census, Sunol has a population of 922 people, where 22.3% of all households include children under the age of 18. The governing body of the Sunol Glen Unified School District (the School Board), a member of the Citizen's Bond Oversight Committee, a retired school principal, and the Facilities Manager of Sunol Glen USD submitted an argument in favor of this measure to the County.
The Sunol Unified School District only school, Sunol Glen School, was built in 1925. Sunol Glen School's renovation and retrofitting process, mostly of its Main Building, began in 2022 and is still ongoing. This bond measure authorizes SGUSD to use property taxes to finish retrofitting the Main Building, modernize and renovate classrooms, and upgrade or replace old infrastructure, including roofs, electrical and HVAC systems, playgrounds, fields, and security fencing. Given the limited scope of bond measure spending, provisions for citizen oversight, and the urgency of repairing and modernizing an 101-year-old building that serves as the only school in Sunol, East Bay Democratic Socialists of America recommends voting YES on this ballot measure.
Measure L - Infrastructure Bond
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure L.
Measure L is a $300M bond measure for infrastructure improvements. It would increase property taxes by approximately $49 annually per $100,000 of assessed value while the bonds are outstanding. The listed priorities for the bond include:
Preventing flooding and combating sea-level rise
Preparing for earthquakes and emergencies
Maintaining rapid response to fires, accidents, and medical emergencies
Repairing potholes and keeping streets safe and accessible
Keeping pollution out of the bay
Alameda currently has around $800M in unfunded infrastructure needs, particularly with respect to climate resilience measures. Alameda is facing threats of rising sea levels due to climate change, which will likely adversely impact a significant number of residents absent stronger resiliency measures.
The measure is subject to an independent citizens’ oversight committee and annual audits. This measure would include police facilities as eligible for infrastructure developments, it does not authorize spending on, for example, police salaries.
East Bay Democratic Socialists of America recommends voting YES on this ballot measure.
Measure M - Charter Amendment
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure M.
Measure M institutes a litany of changes to the Charter of Alameda. Many of them are largely administrative adjustments that tinker in small ways with the City’s system of governance. The most notable changes are as follows:
The measure would permit non-citizens to serve on City boards. This priority is in line with DSA’s Working-Class Democracy platform.
The measure would require certain types of technical expertise on various boards. In particular, it would remove mining expertise as a qualifying factor for at least one seat on the Public Utilities Board, to replace it with environmental expertise.
The measure would remove the City Manager’s management over the City Attorney’s and City Clerks staff, toward redirecting management City Attorney staff by the City Attorney. The same is true in parallel for City Clerk staff.
The measure permits City Councilmembers to employ staff. Electoral staff don’t have a neutral political role -- they can and often are used as tools of patronage. In a socialist model of governance, they are a way of increasing the connectivity between elected officials and working class people, including and especially through direct work with an organization like DSA.
On balance, this is a measure that redirects governance in minor, insufficient ways toward socialist priorities: by expanding influence of non-citizens in governance, mandating technical and environmental expertise, allowing Councilmembers to hire staff, and clarifying outdated, administrative laws that complicate the administration of government.
Measure N - Charter Amendment / Public Land
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA issues no recommendation on Measure N.
Measure N amends the city’s public lands provisions to permit two core changes:
Sales and leases of public land exceeding one year would be approved by resolution rather than by ordinance. This is a technical change that functionally means that any public lands would be subject to one Council meeting, instead of two, and take effect immediately after the first reading, as opposed to thirty days following.
Lowers the approval threshold from four Council votes to three for any leases of city property for ten years or less.
Put simply, this is a measure that would increase Council’s control over public land use and eliminate the need for a supermajority of Councilmembers to approve a lease under ten years. This change would allow the city to better compete with private, for-profit landlords.
Measure O - Zoning Changes
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA issues no recommendation on Measure O.
Measure O would remove the requirement that zoning changes must be approved by a verified petition of nearby voters. This is a trade-off between representative democracy and direct democracy. In practice, standing law can favor approaches that restrict affordable housing development and can make it difficult or impossible for the city government to meet its housing goals. The text of the measure still requires CEQA review and public hearings with notice for any zoning changes, so residents still have influence over the process, if not a direct veto.
Measure P - Street Paving & Storm Drain Parcel Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure P.
Measure P would amend the city’s existing (progressive) parcel tax to add some low-income exemptions and make it apply to possessory interests, but reduce the cap on inflationary adjustment from 5% to 3%, making it more likely to fall behind inflation in higher years, like what we’ve seen recently. It also allows the City Council to change the formula for determining the parcel tax rate to account for the likely sale of Golden Gate Fields to the East Bay Regional Parks District, which would remove the property from the tax roll and otherwise cause a major budget deficit. This measure is expected to increase city revenue.
Measure Q - EMS, ALS, & Fire Protection Parcel Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure Q.
Measure Q amends the EMS/fire parcel tax to:
Add life support and fire services as services that can be funded by the tax.
Expand low-income exemptions, including to tenants. This is an important approach for low-income people and advancing housing affordability for tenants and low-income residents.
Apply to possessory interests (ie. public land leased to private entities).
Authorizes annual CPI-based increases to the tax rate. This is a long-term measure to prevent the tax from decreasing in value over time, as inflation rises.
Change the formula if any parcel at least 500,000 sqft becomes exempt. This is likely a response to Golden Gate Fields, as it is one of few 500,000+ sqft developments and is currently undergoing a potential transition that would make it exempt from the existing parcel tax. This is a protective provision, aimed at preventing a loss of around a fifth of the revenue generated annually by the existing parcel tax. For parcels like Golden Gate Fields, the formula both shifts in structure (it splits the tax into two brackets of developed building sqft and undeveloped lot sqft), does not define minimum tax rates but does define higher tax rates, and authorizes the City Council to impose the tax.
Change the formula for determining the parcel tax rate to account for the likely sale of Golden Gate Fields to the East Bay Regional Parks District, which would remove the property from the tax roll and otherwise cause a major budget deficit.
The City estimates that the combined passage of Measures Q and P would impose around $12 per month in new taxes on residents, with carveouts for low-income residents. The City characterizes the tax as one that is necessary to convert the former Golden Gate Fields into a park.
Measure R - Business License Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure R.
Measure R converts Albany’s business license tax from a per-employee tax to a progressive tax on gross revenue. This is an approach to taxation that is generally preferred from a socialist perspective, because it taxes revenue, tends to have stronger impacts on big businesses, and decreases disincentivization for hiring. The gross receipts tax is structured as a progressive tax, meaning it would fall heaviest upon large businesses. This measure is expected to increase city revenue.
Measure S - Real Property Transfer Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure S.
Measure S converts Albany’s real property transfer tax (tax on selling property) from a flat rate of 1.5% to progressive structure of 1.0% - 3.0%, generating around $1M in additional revenue toward the city’s general fund. General fund revenues are unrestricted and can be spent on municipal services ranging from street maintenance to police. This measure would lower the tax rate on smaller property sales and double the tax rate on larger ones. Measure EE - Charter Amendment
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA issues no recommendation on Measure EE.
Measure EE would reform Oakland’s charter – the document that establishes Oakland’s system of government – to modify the powers of the mayor and city council. Measure EE establishes 9 major changes to how the city government works:
Define the mayor as the chief executive of the city
Empower the council to confirm the mayor’s appointees to directors of finance, human resources, public works, and transportation
Empower the council to create an independent budget and legislative analyst’s office to provide fiscal and policy analysis
Affirm councilmembers’ right to interface between constituents and city officials, and obligate city officials to be responsive
Empower the council to hold hearings and issue subpoenas
Empower the mayor to veto individual elements of the council-passed budget, while allowing the council to override vetos
Require councilmembers’ roles to be their sole employment while serving
Empower the public ethics commission to establish salaries for the mayor and councilmembers
Require that publication of ordinances within 15 days of passage.
This is a lot to take in. The charter was last updated in 1998 under mayor Jerry Brown. In the time since, dissatisfaction with the outcome of those changes has become fairly widespread, with ideological opponents in government routinely agreeing that changes are needed, though often disagreeing on whether that means giving more power to the mayor or the council. Measure EE is the product of years of back-and-forth debate among officials and analysts overseen by Mayor Barbara Lee. Many of these items are controversial, with strong, good-faith arguments in multiple directions, even among socialists.
For instance, many socialists oppose the consolidation of power among mayors, as opposed to diffusing power among councils, which is arguably more representative and less hierarchical. However others may point to the recent tenure of Mayor Mamdani in New York as demonstration that a strong mayoral authority can be a highly effective means to translate public will into a transformative city-wide agenda.
Overall, the proposed changes contain no obvious poison pills designed to strip voters of their power. The most controversial element is the mayoral line-item veto, but taken as a whole most of these changes are positive: for instance, service on Oakland’s city council should not be a side-job, and council members should have no other employment. Additionally, the current restrictions on council members ability to oversee departments on the behalf of their constituents is widely derided across political alignments.
DSA generally favors clear democratic accountability through the legislative branch rather than separately elected executive offices with competing mandates. Similarly, socialists generally oppose giving the chief executive the ability to veto the decisions of the legislative branch.

This bond would serve to improve facilities at six schools, spanning preschool to grade 12 in Winters and unincorporated Solano and Yolo counties. School facilities need to be upgraded to meet safety, technology and educational standards. Funding public schools is of the utmost importance to supporting working communities.
Measure V - Sales Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA issues no recommendation on Measure V.
Measure V was placed on the ballot by the City Council and would establish a 1% sales tax increase in Vacaville, from 8.125% to 9.125%, to be spent on general City services, which includes police, fire protection, emergency response, park and street maintenance, and more. Vacaville’s current sales tax is currently the lowest in Solano County. It would generate approximately $28M annually, by increasing taxes for citizens $500 to $650 each/year. The city is facing a structural deficit, set to require nearly $9M in cuts to municipal services annually over the next five years, so this tax would both patch a deficit and increase revenue.
Currently, the city spends around 67% of its general fund on police and fire, far higher than the statewide average and has cut homeless services due to a budget shortfall. Absent this measure, the city faces a budget deficit, which they have not yet laid out plans for addressing.
The City Council considered putting forward a business license tax, which would have shifted the tax burden for this measure from residents to businesses. This is an approach to taxation that is strongly preferred by socialists, as it functions as a tax on capital and profit, rather than a tax on working people.
Sales taxes are regressive taxes, meaning they not only fall more heavily upon working people, but they also most burden the poorest residents of a city. Funding from this tax would go toward the city’s general fund, meaning it will likely be spent on city services like policing, just as it may be spent on services like affordable housing and parks maintenance.
On balance, this is a regressive tax which would be spent on municipal services, including policing. Absent the passage of this tax, the city will face a deficit that is likely to reduce city services, potentially also including policing. Voters may choose to vote YES to maintain city services, or vote NO in opposition to municipal funding schemes that disproportionately burden low-income, working class people.
Measure X - Revised Business License Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure X.
Measure X would revise the city’s business license tax to reduce business licenses on general businesses and increase taxes on property rental companies, industrial companies, and fossil fuel companies.
The current business tax ranges from $0.55 to $0.75 per $1000 of revenue based on business type. The new proposed rates are:
$0.25 per $1,000 for General and Retail businesses
$0.75 per $1,000 for Contractors
$0.75 per $1,000 for Services businesses
$1.00 per $1,000 for Industrial businesses
$1.00 per $1,000 for Professional businesses
$1.00 per $1,000 for Property Rental businesses
$1.25 per $1,000 for Petroleum businesses
There is an exemption for businesses which make less than $100,000 in revenue. This will mean that around 1,800 smaller businesses will not be taxed. The tax on larger businesses is capped at $25K, with the exception of marine terminal businesses, which are taxed at a higher rate of 3%. Marine terminal businesses, largely for port-related activity, alone will likely constitute more than two-thirds of the revenue from this measure. The measure also eliminates a $7.50 per personal employee tax, which it shifts onto businesses. The revenue from this tax will be allocated to Benicia’s general fund.
Measure Y - Limited City Charter
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure Y.
Measure Y would establish Benicia as a Charter City. The primary stated purpose is to enable the city to adopt a transfer tax.
Charter cities are cities with a city charter. This includes most major cities in California: Los Angeles, San Francisco, Sacramento, Oakland, Berkeley, Hayward, Folsom, Downey, etc. Not every city you can think of is a charter city, but most are. A city with no charter is known as a ‘general law city’.
Measure Y would make Benicia a limited city, specifically to impose a transfer tax through Measure Z. Passage of this measure is necessary to pass a progressive real estate transfer tax (Measure Z, see below) in Benicia. The Napa Solano Central Labor Council endorses Measure Y.
Measure Z - Progressive Real Estate Transfer Tax
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure Z.
Measure Z would impose a progressive transfer tax on real estate sales. A ‘transfer tax’ is just the formal name for sales tax on real estate. Because Benicia is currently a general law city, the city imposes the transfer tax set by the state of California for general law cities, which is 0.055%, or $ 0.55 per $1,000. If Measure Z passes (as well as Measure Y, authorizing Measure Z), the city would begin imposing the following transfer taxes:
For property sales of $2 million or less: $4 per $1,000 (0.4%)
For property sales between $2 million - $10M: $6 per $1,000 (0.6%)
For property sales above $10 million: $8 per $1,000 (0.8%)
This tax is expected to generate $230,000 annually for use in the city’s general fund, going up to around $3.5M annually by 2050. The Napa Solano Central Labor Council endorses Measure Z.
Transfer taxes constitute a tax on the sale of property, which when implemented as a progressive tax, tends to function as a means of taxing wealth. In general, property sales are undertaxed compared to essential goods, like groceries and gas. These transfer taxes are very, very modest. Transfer taxes in Oakland, for instance, range from 1% to 2.5%.

Measure Y – Appropriations Limit / Gann Limit
Reviewed Copy link Print race
TL;DR: East Bay DSA recommends a YES vote on Measure Y.
Measure Y proposes increasing the City’s appropriations limit by $3,000,000, plus 25% of the previous year’s hotel tax revenue, every year for four years, starting with fiscal year 2027-28 to utilize hotel and tourist taxes for public services, parks and recreation, police, and fire response. This does not increase taxes but allows the City to spend tax revenue they are otherwise barred from using due to the Gann limit.
Prop 4, also known as the Gann limit, caps city spending based on population and cost of living. Yountville’s population has declined since 2010 while tourism has grown, and Prop 4 limits Yountville’s access to collected tax revenue. Similar measures passed in 2018 (expired in 2022) and 2024 (expiring in 2027).

This guide is researched and produced by East Bay DSA volunteers and the Electoral Strategy Working Group. The Electoral Strategy Working Group organizes East Bay DSA’s electoral campaigns, and coordinates with endorsed officials. We cannot vote our way to socialism, but the state is nonetheless an arena of struggle against capital, and electoral campaigns can be tools to foster working-class militancy and build socialist power and organization.
