2026 Campaign Endorsements
At EBHO, we don’t only think about Election Day when it’s getting close to the first in November – we have ballot measures on the brain all year long, and we know many of you do too!
That’s why we are so excited to announce a few endorsements and invite YOU to get involved early and often in what we think will be some of the most consequential issues in this year’s general election. From signature gathering for a citizen’s initiative, to formally endorsing, from spreading awareness to calling your state representatives, there are many ways you can support these critical measures from now until .
Connect Bay Area Transit: A Regional Transportation Ballot Measure to reimagine and reinvest in a system that serves today’s riders and inspires the next generation.
Roadmap Home 2030: A bold, long-term plan to create the future we want for California by building affordable homes, protecting low-income renters, ending homelessness, and advancing racial equity and economic inclusion.
Proposition 1 (Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond Act of 2026): A statewide bond that would invest $11.25 billion in building and preserving affordable homes while expanding homeownership.
NO on Proposition 43: Prop 43 makes it harder for you to fund critical services like police and fire departments, fix and repair local roads, staff our local schools or backfill federal healthcare cuts.
City of Berkeley Local Measure X: An amendment to the City of Berkeley’s Rent Stabilization and Eviction for Just Cause Ordinance by making most rent control and registration exemptions inapplicable to units that were not exempt when the current tenancy began; allowing modification or waiver of fees for subsidized units owned or leased by nonprofits; changing the annual registration deadline; limiting annual rent increases to ten percent; and allowing tenant associations in smaller properties, be adopted.

Connect Bay Area Transit
The Regional Transit Measure (also known as Connect Bay Area, or SB 63), is a transit funding ballot measure for San Francisco, Alameda, Contra Costa, Santa Clara, and San Mateo Counties. If passed, it will provide funding for our transit services, including BART, Caltrain, AC Transit, SamTrans, VTA, and others, and prevent major service cuts and station closures.
EBHO is partnering with Transform and housing leaders from across the region on cohost a webinar on the Regional Transit Measure. Join us to hear how to support the regional transit funding measure and why saving transit matters so much to the affordable housing community.
Register for How Housers Can Help Save Transit
On , The Connect Bay Area campaign announced it had submitted more than 305,000 signatures to qualify a regional transit funding measure for the November ballot — blowing past the 186,000 valid signatures required to appear on the November ballot. We would like to give a special shout out to EBHO’s very own Board Member Cathy Eberhardt who collected over 1000 signatures, and Executive Director Lindsay Haddix and Chief Program Officer Rev. Sophia DeWitt for both collecting 50!
The success of this effort is built on one of the largest grassroots transit organizing efforts the region has ever seen and major support from business and labor organizations. Now, we need to gear up for phase two of the campaign so we can get the measure passed in November.
This funding measure is designed to prevent catastrophic transit service cuts and provide reliable operating funding for major transit systems and local bus operators throughout the region. The measure also funds transit improvements, capital projects within counties, and targeted road improvements.
Without a sustainable funding solution, Bay Area transit agencies face devastating service reductions, including:
BART: Elimination of two full lines, no service after on weekdays, and no
weekend service;
SF Muni: Elimination of entire routes, 50% cuts to major bus and metro lines, and severe reductions to historic streetcar and cable car service;
Caltrain: Hourly weekday service, no service after , and no weekend service;
AC Transit: Up to 37% overall service cuts.

Roadmap Home 2030
The Roadmap Home 2030 is a bold, long-term plan to create the future we want for California by building affordable homes, protecting low-income renters, ending homelessness, and advancing racial equity and economic inclusion.
Developed with partners across diverse sectors, the Roadmap Home 2030 is a
comprehensive, multi-year policy platform that provides near- and long-term policy
solutions. If enacted, these solutions can realize California’s promise and create a
future where every Californian has the essential foundation necessary for health
opportunity, and wellbeing: a safe, affordable place to call home.

Proposition 1: Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond Act of 2026
Thanks to our collective dedication and unwavering advocacy, on , Governor Gavin Newsom officially signed a $11.25 billion bond dedicated to transforming the housing landscape across our state is headed to the ballot.
The Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond Act of 2026 (SB 417) is a generation-defining measure that will support California’s working families, seniors, veterans, farmworkers, university students, first-time homebuyers, and unhoused Californians. It places a $11.25 billion bond on this year’s ballot — allowing voters to support the production and preservation of affordable housing for lower-income households.
Other measures on the ballot could negatively impact housing. Proposition 43, the “The Taxpayer Deception Initiative” (our label), would require that local ballot measures proposing, extending, or increasing special taxes initiated by citizens receive a two-thirds (66.67%) vote to be enacted. If this measure passes, it will make California the hardest place in the country to raise local revenue for vital public services.
NO on Proposition 43
Prop 43 rewrites the rules of our democracy. Today, when your community votes to fund something local — affordable housing, a neighborhood school, a road repair, or your local fire department — the majority decides. Prop 43 changes that. It lets just over one-third of voters block what nearly two-thirds want. Under Prop 43, a NO vote counts twice as much as a YES. The winners are the corporate special interests who’d rather not pay their fair share.

City of Berkeley Local Measure X
Placed on the ballot by unanimous vote of the City Council to make common-sense tweaks to the Berkeley Rent Stabilization Ordinance, Measure X will:
Enable the Rent Board to waive fees for non-profit affordable housing providers.
Align Berkeley with state law by setting an overall maximum 10% cap on annual rent increases.
Ensure that tenants who begin their tenancy with protections are not stripped of their protections arbitrarily, while still allowing small landlords to access exemptions for future tenancies.
Enable, but not require, the City Council to enact programs to create new rent-controlled housing stock.
Close loopholes used by corporate landlords to undermine tenant unions.
Make administrative changes to the Ordinance to increase efficiency.
Measure X was crafted through a collaborative process between the Rent Board, the City Council, tenant advocates, non-profit affordable housing providers, and landlords to ensure a final product that was acceptable to all parties. Measure X represents the first time in over a decade that a measure to amend the Rent Stabilization Ordinance passed Council unanimously.
Passing Measure X will reduce financial burdens on non-profit affordable housing providers who provide key services to low income and elderly Berkeley residents, all while maintaining existing legal protections for vulnerable residents. It will also enable the Rent Board to provide more efficient and effective services to landlords and tenants alike by modernizing and streamlining administrative processes.
